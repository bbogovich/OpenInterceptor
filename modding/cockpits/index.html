    Cockpits (HD and 3D)¶

    A cockpit is an entry of the cockpits: section of a pack's rules; a ship uses it with cockpit: <id>. It can be 2D art, a 3D model, or both, and it says where the instruments go — as rectangles in a 2D design space, and for a 3D cockpit optionally as anchors on the model's surface, drawn in perspective.

    cockpits:
  a:
    image: original:CockpitA.dll#12000   # 2D art: original, a pack image, or none
    design: [640, 480]                   # coordinate space of instruments (default 640, 480)
    model: models/cockpit.obj            # optional 3D cockpit around the pilot's eye
    model_offset: [0, 0, 0]              # metres from the eye (x right, y up, -z ahead)
    model_scale: 1
    instruments:
      message: [118, 12, 410, 24]
      radar_front: [111, 332, 60, 60]    # the round glass of the scope
      radar_rear: [473, 332, 60, 60]
      mfd: [246, 338, 156, 82]
      gauge1: [243, 299, 26, 26]         # speed
      gauge2: [288, 299, 26, 26]         # energy
      gauge3: [333, 299, 26, 26]         # shields
      gauge4: [378, 299, 26, 26]         # hull
    Key Default Meaning
    image — Asset reference of the 2D art, drawn over the whole design space; none for no 2D art.
    design [640, 480] The size of the coordinate space the instruments use. The art is stretched to it whatever its pixel size.
    model none Asset reference of a 3D cockpit model.
    model_offset [0, 0, 0] Where the model's origin sits relative to the pilot's eye, in metres.
    model_scale 1 Model units → metres.
    instruments — name: [x, y, w, h] in design space. Names: message, radar_front, radar_rear, mfd, gauge1–gauge4, power, systems, status (the throttle/speed line; without it the line is plain screen text at the top left). A rectangle more than five times wider than tall is a strip and gets the instrument's one-line form (below).
    anchors none 3D cockpits: name: {at, normal, size} — the instrument drawn in perspective on the model (below).
    wide none Wide screens: side bands beside 4:3 art: {blackout: true, color: [0,0,0], from: 305, left: images/left.png, right: images/right.png}. The X-COM cockpits black out the dashboard sides from the height where each image's edges become solid (A 305, B 382, C 352) (R-HUD-013).

    All instrument text — MFD pages, the message line, gauge labels, the power and systems labels — is sized to fit its instrument's box, so a small rectangle gets small text rather than text spilling out of it.

    Shield rings¶

    Each radar scope carries a ring just inside its edge showing a shield facing: the front scope the fore shield, the rear scope the aft. Green above 66 %, amber above 33 %, red below, blinking red when the facing is down. Size the radar_front / radar_rear rectangles to the scope glass and the ring sits on its rim.

    The power and systems instruments (Stage 2)¶

    Instrument Shows
    power Four columns: B-<level> and S-<level> (beam and shield charge setting, A–E, one cell lit per level), then F and A bars for the fore and aft shields as a fraction of their balanced capacity (red below 25 %; the favoured facing's label is white). When the power budget is fully used, used/budget appears in yellow.
    status The throttle, speed and drive/system flags line (THR 20% -274 m/s VCT BURN …), fitted to the rectangle and clipped with .. when long.
    strips In a strip, power reads B-<level> S-<level> F<fore %> A<aft %> (red when a facing is below 25 %), and systems lists only the systems that are not plain OK in their lamp colours, or SYSTEMS OK. The interceptor cockpits put status, power and systems in the two strips of the top display bar.
    systems One lamp per fitted system with a three-letter label (ENG, MAN, SEN, SHD, HYP, VCT, TRC, CLK, ECM, DCC): green working, bright cyan switched on, amber damaged, red knocked out (blinking while being repaired), dark destroyed.

    A cockpit that does not place them still gets them: small panels at the top right of the cockpit's own design space (from 87.5 % of the design width, 9 % of its height down), with a dark backdrop over cockpit art. The original cockpits and the interceptor pack do not place them, so they appear there; the default campaign's 3D cockpit has bezels for both.

    Radar rectangles enclose the scope's glass. Contacts are placed in the circle inscribed in the rectangle, so draw it tight around the round glass (centre on the painted crosshair). Over cockpit art the engine draws only the contacts — the art supplies the scope rings; on the glass HUD (no art) the engine adds two rings and an F/R label. The interceptor pack's values were measured from the original art to ±1 px.

    A cockpit needs an image, a model, or both. Without a cockpit (or in the non-cockpit views) the HUD uses glass instruments in a default layout.

    How the screen is fitted¶

    The design space is scaled as large as fits the window and centred (Layout::new(screen, design)): a 640×480 cockpit fills the height of a 16:9 window with space visible either side; a 1920×1080 design fills it exactly. So:

    • An HD repaint at the same proportions (2560×1920 for 640×480) needs no rule changes at all: just override the image.
    • A widescreen redesign sets design: [1920, 1080] and new instruments rectangles.

    A 3D cockpit instead of the overlay¶

    With model, the cockpit is a 3D model drawn around the pilot's eye in the cockpit view (F1). It is drawn after the world with a fresh depth buffer, so the world never pokes through it, and with a 5 cm near plane, so struts right in front of the eye are not clipped. Model it in metres around the eye at the origin, +Y up, looking along −Z.

    Its instruments can be placed two ways:

    2D rectangle (instruments) Anchor (anchors)
    Drawn flat, over the view, at its design-space position into a texture, then mapped onto a rectangle on the model's surface, in perspective
    Window shape depends on the field of view matching the design (below) sits in its bezel at any window shape and field of view
    Used for 2D cockpits; any instrument without an anchor 3D cockpits with screens or bezels on the dashboard

    An anchor is {at: [x, y, z], normal: [x, y, z], size: [w, h]} in metres from the eye: the centre of the instrument on the model, the surface's outward normal, and the instrument's width and height on the surface. Its "up" is world up projected onto the surface. An instrument with an anchor is drawn only on the surface in the cockpit view; its 2D rectangle still serves the other views and the fallback.

    Field of view. The 3D view normally has a 60° vertical field. On a window narrower than the cockpit's design proportions (a 4:3 window with a 16:9 cockpit), that would cut off the cockpit's sides and misplace 2D instruments relative to their bezels, so the field widens until the design's full width fits (app::flight::cockpit_fov). Windows as wide as the design or wider keep 60°. Measure 2D rectangles for the camera they will be seen with: eye at the origin, looking −Z, 60° vertical field, at the design aspect. Anchored instruments do not need this.

    The default campaign's Guard rockets fly from one, an art-deco dashboard (original-free, generated by tools/default_assets/models.py). The generator writes both placements, measured from the bezels it builds, to models/guard_cockpit.instruments.yaml, from which they are copied into the rules:

    cockpits:
  guard:
    image: none
    model: models/guard_cockpit.obj
    model_offset: [0, 0, 0]
    design: [1280, 720]
    instruments:
      message: [404, 474, 472, 26]
      radar_front: [101, 540, 155, 156]
      mfd: [548, 514, 184, 186]
      power: [292, 628, 196, 64]
      systems: [792, 632, 196, 32]
      # … radar_rear, gauge1–gauge4
    anchors:
      message: {at: [0, -0.1729, -0.8497], normal: [0, 0.342, 0.9397], size: [0.6449, 0.0351]}
      mfd: {at: [0, -0.311, -0.7995], normal: [0, 0.342, 0.9397], size: [0.2445, 0.2218]}
      # … one per instrument
ships:
  comet:
    cockpit: guard

    To give an original ship a 3D cockpit, a mod adds only:

    cockpits:
  a:
    image: none                    # or keep the art and add the model on top
    model: models/lightning_pit.obj
    design: [1280, 720]
    # … new instruments in 1280×720, and anchors (or place them with F12, below)

    The cockpit layout editor (F12)¶

    In the cockpit view of any ship with a cockpit, F12 opens the layout editor; the battle pauses while it is open.

    Key / mouse Effect
    Tab / Shift+Tab Select the next / previous instrument (its name is shown at the top).
    2D instrument: drag inside Move it.
    2D instrument: drag the corner Resize it.
    2D instrument: arrow keys Nudge 1 design pixel (Shift: 10).
    3D (anchored) instrument: click the dashboard Place it there: a ray from the eye through the cursor is intersected with the cockpit model; the hit point becomes the anchor's centre and the hit face's normal its facing.
    Mouse wheel, + / - Grow / shrink the selected instrument by 5 % (2D about its centre; 3D keeps its proportions).
    S Save.
    F12 or Esc Leave the editor (unsaved changes stay for this flight only).

    Save writes a small addon into the user's mods folder, <user data>/mods/<campaign>-cockpit-<cockpit id>/, with a pack.yaml (kind: addon, targets: [<campaign>]) and rules/cockpit-layout.yaml holding the cockpit's instruments and anchors. It loads with that campaign from then on and overrides the original layout key by key; delete the folder (or disable the pack) to go back. To ship a layout with your own mod, copy cockpit-layout.yaml into its rules/.

    Status: the 3D cockpit path, anchors and the editor are implemented; the on-screen checks are manual tests MT-2-14 to MT-2-17.

    Open a model straight in the viewer: openinterceptor --model <ship id or mesh name>. In the viewer, W cycles solid / solid + wireframe / wireframe, [ ] select faces (Shift: ten at a time) and show each face's number, corner count, texture, flags, overlay layer and UVs, I labels every face towards the camera, and O hides overlay-flagged faces.
