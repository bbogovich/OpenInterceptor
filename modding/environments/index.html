    Environments and terrain¶

    Where a battle happens: deep space, in orbit over a planet, in a small "pocket", or in the air above a planet's surface with ground installations on it. Environments and terrains are rules data (app 0.6.0; ground installations 0.6.1), so a mod adds worlds without code. A mission or scenario picks one with environment: <id>; without it the battle is in open space, exactly as before.

    Every key with its type and default: Mod file reference › environments and terrains. Requirements: R-MSN-008, R-FLT-011, R-FLT-012, R-HUD-012. Hazards (asteroids, mines, black holes, ion storms) are separate and combine with any environment: Missions › Hazards.

    A worked example¶

    The default campaign's resources/default/rules/environments.yaml defines four environments and two terrains:

    environments:
  orbit:
    # High orbit over a blue world: open-space physics, a planet in the sky.
    backdrop: {planet: {color: [0.30, 0.52, 0.95], size: 48, direction: [0.25, -0.55, -1]}}
  pocket:
    # A small, enclosed arena: tight, close-quarters fights.
    arena: 12km
    backdrop: {planet: {color: [0.55, 0.25, 0.60], size: 90, direction: [-0.3, 0.2, 1]}}
  moon_surface:
    # A cratered moon: thin air, low gravity, a dark violet sky with stars showing.
    physics: atmosphere
    gravity: 3.7
    air_density: 0.35
    ceiling: 9km
    terrain: craters
    sky: {top: [0.03, 0.02, 0.08], horizon: [0.32, 0.24, 0.34], fog: [0.30, 0.23, 0.32], fog_distance: 14km, stars: 0.6}
  dusk_hills:
    # Rolling hills at dusk: thick air, full gravity, a warm sky. Rockets need speed to fly.
    physics: atmosphere
    gravity: 9.8
    air_density: 1.0
    ceiling: 7km
    terrain: hills
    sky: {top: [0.10, 0.12, 0.30], horizon: [0.95, 0.55, 0.35], fog: [0.80, 0.50, 0.38], fog_distance: 10km, stars: 0.1}

terrains:
  craters:
    generate: {kind: craters, seed: 7}
    size: 30km
    height: [0, 1600]
    colors: {low: [0.30, 0.27, 0.26], high: [0.62, 0.58, 0.55], slope: [0.20, 0.18, 0.18]}
  hills:
    generate: {kind: hills, seed: 3}
    size: 30km
    height: [0, 1400]
    colors: {low: [0.22, 0.33, 0.16], high: [0.55, 0.50, 0.32], slope: [0.36, 0.30, 0.22]}

    A scenario on the moon:

    # resources/default/scenarios/surface-moon.yaml (abridged)
id: surface-moon
player: choose
environment: moon_surface
player_position: [0, 2600, 6000]     # 2.6 km up: start well above the highest ground
objects:
  raider1: {class: raider_saucer, ai: enemy, position: [-800, 2400, 1000], speed: 200}

    Space environments¶

    With physics: space (the default) the campaign's flight model is unchanged. Two keys change the battle:

    • arena: the arena radius for battles here, replacing flight.arena_radius — a pocket is a small arena with its edge close by ("ARENA BOUNDARY").
    • backdrop: {planet: {color, size, direction}}: a planet hanging in the sky, size degrees across, in direction, lit from the sun's side with a darker limb. It is drawn behind everything, like the stars.

    Atmospheric flight¶

    With physics: atmosphere every craft that can move feels, each tick (ρ = air_density, g = gravity):

    Force Rule Effect
    Drag a = −k · ρ · |v| · v, with k = acceleration / atmosphere_max_speed² Full thrust balances drag at atmosphere_max_speed in air of density 1. Without thrust a craft slows down. Thinner air (ρ < 1) lets it go faster.
    Gravity g along −Y, except for hover: true craft A winged craft must hold altitude with lift; a hovering saucer stays up on its drive.
    Lift g · ρ · (forward speed / lift)² along the craft's own up, capped at 2g At the lift speed the wings carry the weight exactly; slower the craft sinks, faster it climbs. Banking tilts the lift, which is how a winged craft turns.
    Ground the terrain's height under the craft A touchdown at no more than 25 m/s into the ground while upright is a landing: the craft stops sinking and slides to a halt. Faster, or not upright (up more than about 45° off the ground's normal), is a crash: damage of (impact speed)² × 0.5, a collision sound and a bounce that keeps half the speed.
    Ceiling the environment's ceiling Climbing above it leaves the battle, as a hyperspace jump does ("LEFT THE ATMOSPHERE").

    Ship keys that tune it (every ship class; reference):

    Key Default Meaning
    atmosphere_max_speed 60 % of max_speed Top speed in air of density 1, m/s.
    lift 120 for fighter, 0 for every other kind The wing speed that carries the weight, m/s; 0 = no wings.
    hover false Gravity does not pull it down. The default campaign's saucers, dreadnought, robot sentinel, grappler and cargo orb hover; the rockets fly on wings.
    atmospheric true Can enter an atmosphere; read but not yet acted on.

    Choosing numbers. A craft's stall speed is its lift speed scaled by the air: level flight needs forward speed = lift / √ρ. In dusk_hills (ρ = 1) a fighter with lift: 120 must fly at 120 m/s or more to hold height; on the moon (ρ = 0.35, g = 3.7) the same wings need about 203 m/s, but gravity is weak, so sinking is slow. Its top speed in the moon's thin air is atmosphere_max_speed / √ρ ≈ 1.7 × as fast as in thick air.

    Simple physics (R-FLT-013) keeps its arcade handling in an atmosphere: the forces act, but the craft's velocity is still set along its nose each tick.

    AI pilots pull up to a climb at a speed their wings can fly when they are below 150 m, or when their vertical speed would take them below 100 m within 3 s.

    Terrains¶

    A terrain is a square heightfield: a grid of resolution × resolution heights (default 129, 17 – 257), size metres across (default 30 km), centred on the origin. Beyond the square the edge heights continue. The heights span height: [low, high] metres. They come from exactly one of:

    • generate: {kind, seed} — hills: smooth value noise summed over five octaves (rolling hills); craters: a gently rolling plain with about sixty bowl-shaped craters with raised rims, a few large and many small. The same seed always gives the same ground. Built once, while the rules are read.
    • heightmap: textures/terrain/my_world.png — a greyscale PNG or TGA from your pack: black is low, white is high (the red channel is read). Any size; it is resampled to resolution. Built once, when the content loads.

    The ground height under a craft is interpolated between the four grid points around it (bilinear), so it has no steps. The terrain is drawn as one mesh, coloured from colors: {low, high, slope}: from low to high by height, blended towards slope where the ground is steep, with a slight per-point variation so wide flats do not look painted. It is lit by the sun and fades into the fog with distance.

    Higher resolution means more detail and a heavier mesh: 129 points per side is about 32 000 triangles; 257 is about 131 000.

    Not built yet: detail textures (texture: {base, detail}) and scattered objects such as rocks (objects:) — the design is on Format: missions, environments, events.

    Sky and fog¶

    sky: replaces black space:

    • a dome around the camera coloured from top (straight up) to horizon (at and below the horizon), drawn behind everything; the screen is cleared to the horizon colour;
    • stars (0–1) dims the starfield seen through the sky (0 = no stars, the default);
    • distant things fade into fog (default: the horizon colour). The fade grows with the square of the distance: a quarter at half fog_distance, complete at fog_distance (default 15 km). Make fog_distance shorter than half the terrain's size, so its edge stays hidden.

    Ground installations¶

    An object class of kind: ground is a ground installation: it never moves, and it is set down on the terrain wherever it is placed, at the ground height plus a quarter of its collision radius — the y of its position is replaced. The environment is set before anything is placed, so this works for scenario objects and mission roles alike. In space a ground object stays where it is placed.

    Anti-air is simply a turret on the ground: a kind: ground class with beams, a turret_arc and a turret behaviour. The default campaign's resources/default/rules/ground.yaml:

    objects:
  guard_colony:
    name: "@object.guard_colony.name"
    model_set: mining_facility
    kind: ground
    faction: guard
    hit_points: 6000
    shields: 800
    armor: 300
  dominion_flak:                 # an anti-air battery
    name: "@object.dominion_flak.name"
    model_set: dominion_turret_pod
    kind: ground
    faction: dominion
    hit_points: 400
    armor: 120
    beams: [death_ray]
    turret_arc: 180
    behaviour: station_turret

    The HUD in an atmosphere¶

    Over a ground the status line adds ALT 1240 VS -35: height above the terrain directly below, and vertical speed (+ up). The forward view shows an artificial horizon — a level line straight ahead in your compass direction, whatever your pitch and roll — and a pitch ladder with labelled rungs at −20°, −10°, +10° and +20°. In space neither is shown.

    Testbed scenarios¶

    Scenario (default campaign) Environment What to try
    env-orbit orbit The planet backdrop; a dogfight with two scout saucers.
    env-pocket pocket The 12 km arena edge; two Black Comet hot-rods.
    surface-moon moon_surface Low gravity, thin air, craters, the violet sky with stars; landing and crashing.
    surface-hills dusk_hills Full gravity and thick air: rockets need speed to stay up; the warm dusk sky and fog; the ceiling at 7 km.
    surface-colony moon_surface Defend a Guard colony (protected) on the ground from two raider saucers.
    surface-strike dusk_hills Knock out three Dominion flak batteries in the hills; they track low fliers.

    The first four were soaked with the AI pilot (app 0.6.0). The interceptor campaign stays in space, as the original did.

    Common errors¶

    Message Cause
    environments.x: an atmosphere needs a terrain physics: atmosphere without terrain:.
    environments.x: unknown terrain 'y' terrain: names no terrains: entry.
    environments.x: physics: 'y' is not space or atmosphere A misspelled physics.
    terrains.x: needs generate or heightmap / give generate or heightmap, not both A terrain must have exactly one source.
    terrains.x: generate.kind: 'y' is not hills or craters An unknown generator.
    terrains.x: heightmap <file>: … The heightmap image could not be loaded.
    mission x: unknown environment 'y' (--check) A mission names an environment no pack defines (a scenario fails to start with unknown environment 'y').
