    Screen layouts¶

    A screen layout says where a screen's parts go. The engine owns what each screen does and what its parts are — the widgets — and gives each a default place; a pack moves the widgets it wants, one at a time (app 0.16.0, plan item 6.1). It is the companion of a skin: the skin decides how things look, the layout where they are.

    Requirement: R-CPN-011. Every key with its type: Mod file reference › screens. How the screen reads it: Architecture › Presentation › Widgets and screen layouts.

    So far one screen is laid out: the strategy screen, starmap.

    The strategy screen's widgets¶

    Widget What it holds
    top The top bar: date, funds and score; the base switcher; pause and the four speeds; Save; Menu.
    map The star map, with the hover label and the legend along its bottom edge.
    nav The ten buttons that choose the list (two rows of five).
    info The selection on the map: its name, its details and up to four action buttons.
    panel The chosen list: its title, the rows, the selected row in full and its action buttons.

    The engine's own layout, in a 1280 × 720 design space:

    screens:
  starmap:
    design: [1280, 720]
    widgets:
      top: [0, 0, 1280, 44]        # x, y, width, height
      map: [8, 52, 790, 660]
      nav: [806, 52, 466, 72]
      info: [806, 132, 466, 212]
      panel: [806, 352, 466, 360]

    Moving a widget¶

    Put a screens section in any rules file of your pack (for example rules/screens.yaml). Name only what you move; the rest keep the engine's places:

    # rules/screens.yaml — the map on the right, the lists on the left
screens:
  starmap:
    widgets:
      map: [482, 52, 790, 660]
      nav: [8, 52, 466, 72]
      info: [8, 132, 466, 212]
      panel: [8, 352, 466, 360]

    A later pack changes a layout the same way, so an addon can move one panel of a campaign's screen without restating the others.

    The design space and the window¶

    The rectangles are written in the layout's design space. Unlike a cockpit, which keeps its proportions and is centred, a screen fills the window: the design space is stretched to the window's width and height separately, so a rectangle at the right edge stays at the right edge on any window shape. Text and button heights scale by the smaller of the two stretches (never below half size), so they stay legible on a small window.

    Write a new design when you lay a screen out from scratch at another size:

    screens:
  starmap:
    design: [1920, 1080]
    widgets:
      top: [0, 0, 1920, 60]
      map: [12, 72, 1200, 996]
      nav: [1224, 72, 684, 96]
      info: [1224, 180, 684, 320]
      panel: [1224, 512, 684, 556]

    When you change design, give every widget a rectangle in the new space: one left out keeps the engine's 1280 × 720 numbers, which are then read in your space.

    Keep the info and panel widgets at least about 300 × 200 design units: they leave room for a nine-slice frame's corners, the title, a few lines and a row of buttons.

    Background¶

    background: images/ui/starmap_frame.png names an image drawn over the whole window, stretched to fit, behind every widget (app 0.16.2; in 0.16.0 it was read but not drawn). It is loaded the first time the screen draws; an image that cannot be loaded is reported once on standard error (screens.starmap.background <file>: …) and the screen falls back to the skin's background colour. Leave room in the picture where the map and panels sit, since they draw over it.

    Not built yet¶

    • Layouts for the other screens: the main menu, the launcher, the credits and the prompts are placed in code.
    • Other screen ids: screens.<id> with another id is read and checked, but no screen uses it yet.
    • Custom panels bound to campaign queries, and new widgets: a layout can move the engine's widgets, not add its own.
    • The interceptor wrapper screens at 640 × 480 from the original art (6.7).

    Errors¶

    Message Cause
    screens.starmap: widgets.x: not one of top, map, nav, info, panel A widget the screen does not have.
    screens.starmap: widgets.x: expected [x, y, w, h] A rectangle without four numbers.
    screens.starmap: design: expected [width, height] design is not two positive numbers.

    Each message is prefixed by the file it came from; --check reports them without a window.
