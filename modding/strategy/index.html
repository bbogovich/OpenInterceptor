    Strategy layer¶

    The campaign around the battles — the star map, bases, research, the economy, craft, pilots and the enemy raids that start battles — is pack data (app 0.7.0 – 0.7.3), and so is the optional logistics layer of mining, manufacturing, research stations and supply convoys (app 0.9.0, Logistics). The story on top of it — events, arcs, the rival's plans, relations, the strategist, endings and the encyclopaedia (app 0.10.0) — has its own page, Story layer. A campaign that has a start: section has a strategy layer: the launcher then leads with Campaign: continue and Campaign: new game (User guide › Campaign). A campaign without start: is battles only, as before.

    Requirements: R-STR-001 – R-STR-027. Design (the full planned format, of which this page is the built part): Format: strategy layer. How the engine plays these rules: Architecture › Campaign.

    Where the files go¶

    Strategy sections are ordinary rules sections, so they may sit in any rules file; by convention they go in <pack>/strategy/*.yaml, which the loader reads like rules/, campaign/ and missions/. The default campaign splits them by subject:

    File Sections
    resources/default/strategy/start.yaml start, flags
    resources/default/strategy/map.yaml maps, star_classes
    resources/default/strategy/research.yaml research_model, research
    resources/default/strategy/economy.yaml economy, incomes, expenses, items, hulls, base_modules, base_types
    resources/default/strategy/raids.yaml raids
    resources/default/strategy/pilots.yaml pilots, scores
    resources/default/strategy/logistics.yaml resources, components, recipes, facility_types, research_sites, logistics; and, merged into the reach map, the hulls and the research tree: deposits, features, hull components, two station-only topics (app 0.9.0)
    resources/default/strategy/story.yaml events, arcs, plans, relations, strategist, endings, campaign_end, encyclopaedia (app 0.10.1; Story layer)

    The interceptor campaign has two (app 0.8.0, below): packs/interceptor/strategy/references.yaml, generated from the player's own files, and packs/interceptor/strategy/campaign.yaml, written by hand; and since app 0.10.2 its story, packs/interceptor/strategy/story.yaml (Story layer).

    Each file starts with # yaml-language-server: $schema=../../../schemas/rules.schema.json, so an editor offers every key below. Definition sections (maps, research, items, …) layer across packs like any other definitions: an addon may add a research topic, inherit an item or delete: true a raid (Rulesets).

    Units¶

    • Times — build_time, every, delivery — take a time unit: 10d (days, new in app 0.6.7 for every time quantity), 36h, 90min; a plain number is hours in the strategy layer.
    • Map units — positions, speed, sensor_range, max_distance — are the map's own units; unit_ly says how many light years one is (for display). speed is map units per hour.
    • Money is a plain number. upkeep, maintenance and salary are per month (720 hours). economy.cost_scale multiplies every item, hull and module cost, sell price, upkeep and maintenance (app 0.8.0; for data in thousands, like the original's).
    • Work units — research size, prod_time — are consumed at a rate per hour: a base's research rate (research_model.rate) or production rate (module effect production_rate).

    Expressions: the campaign context¶

    Prerequisites (requires:), amounts (incomes, expenses) and the research rate are written in the condition language with the campaign context (script::campaign, app 0.6.7). Plain numbers are expressions too. Every id the strategy layer defines — topics, items, hulls, modules, base types, raids, flags — and every faction, ship class and mission id is a named constant, so a misspelt name (research(lazers)) is a load error that names it. Difficulty level ids are constants as well (difficulty >= veteran).

    Query Result Meaning
    research(x) true/false Topic x has been researched.
    researching(x) true/false A base is working on topic x.
    has(x) number Items x in store, all bases.
    module_count(x) number Working modules x — at the base being evaluated (in a research rate), else at all bases.
    bases number The player's bases.
    money number The player's money.
    day number Days since the campaign began.
    month number Whole months (30 days) since the campaign began.
    difficulty number The difficulty level (compare with level names).
    pilots number Pilots on the roster.
    score number The campaign score so far.
    month_score number Score gained since the last monthly report.
    random(p) true/false True with probability p (0..1), from the campaign's seeded generator.
    flag(x) true/false Campaign flag x is set (by a mission outcome's set_flag(x)).
    salvaged(x) true/false Something named x has been salvaged (see Battles).
    craft number The player's craft, all bases.
    arc(x), plan(x), relation(x), fired(x), battles_won number The story's queries (app 0.10.0): see Story layer › Conditions.

    Sections¶

    start¶

    How a new campaign begins. Without start: the campaign has no strategy layer.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    date text required The first day, YYYY-MM-DD (Gregorian calendar, leap years counted). A missing or malformed date is a load error.
    money number 0 Starting money.
    map id the first map The map (maps.<id>).
    bases list of {type, system, name, modules} none Bases the player starts with: a base type, the system it is in, its name, and modules beyond the type's template. Starting bases and their modules are finished.
    craft list of {hull, base, count} none Craft the player starts with; base is an index into bases (0 = the first).
    pilots number 0 Pilots on the starting roster; they take the starting craft in order.
    research ids none Topics already known.
    stock mapping item: count none Items in store at the first base.
    money_from original:<file>/<key> none The starting money read from the player's original file, from the section named after the difficulty level (its id with a capital first letter: veteran → [Veteran]); the key's name is matched without regard to case. Replaces money. Not found = load error (app 0.8.0).

    maps and star_classes¶

    maps: {<id>: …} — the star map.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    kind fixed / generated fixed Hand-made (the systems as written) or generated when a campaign starts.
    systems fixed: mapping id: {name, pos, class, hidden}; generated: [min, max] or a count — Fixed: each system's display name (default the id), pos: [x, y] (required), star class (default yellow) and hidden (not shown until a base's sensors reach it; default false). Generated: how many systems (default 30).
    lanes list of {between: [a, b]} none Lanes between two of the map's systems (drawn on the map).
    travel free / lanes free Straight lines anywhere (the original) or along the lanes (app 0.7.3; see Travel).
    size [w, h] [60000, 60000] Generated: the rectangle, map units.
    names list of text none Generated: system names to draw from (then numbered).
    seed number the campaign's Generated: a fixed seed, so every campaign gets the same map.
    black_holes number 1 Generated: how many systems are hidden black holes.
    unit_ly number 0.001 Light years per map unit (display only).

    Fixed-map systems also take bodies (a list of planets, moons and belts; descriptive) and, for a campaign with logistics, deposits and features (app 0.9.0).

    star_classes: {<id>: {color: [r, g, b], weight}} — how a star of that class is drawn, and (for generated maps) how often it appears relative to the others (weight, default 1; 0 = never rolled). Generated systems are scattered at least a little apart, take a class by weight and a name from names.

    research and research_model¶

    research: {<id>: …} — the research tree.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Display name.
    size number 1000 Work units to finish (also read from the original's file_size, in thousands, through inheritance).
    requires condition none Available to research only while this holds: research(x), salvaged(x), any campaign query.
    unlocks list none What finishing it makes available: items.<id>, hulls.<id>, modules.<id> (or base_modules.<id>), base_types.<id>. Something a topic unlocks cannot be bought, made or built until the topic is known.
    description text empty Encyclopaedia text (Stage 5 shows it).
    prereq1, prereq2, prereq3 numbers none The original's numeric prerequisites, read through inheritance; with a tech_ids table and no requires, they become research(a) and research(b) (app 0.8.0).
    lab, site, start_cost none A station-only topic: see Logistics › research stations (app 0.9.0).

    research_model: — how research progresses.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    mode download / labs download How it is described: the original's downloads, or staff in labs. The rule is the same: each base works on one topic at a time.
    rate expression 10 Work units per hour at one base, evaluated per base (so module_count counts that base's modules).
    switching keep_progress / lose_progress keep_progress Whether switching a base to another topic keeps the work done on the old one (with lose_progress it is lost unless another base is still working on it).

    A topic finishes when its work reaches size; every base working on it stops, and a message says so.

    items and hulls¶

    items: {<id>: …} — equipment, ammunition and goods.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Display name.
    kind id goods beam, launcher, missile, armor, shield, engine, sensor, special, probe, mine or goods; matched against hull slots.
    cost number 0 Purchase price; 0 = cannot be bought.
    sell_price number 0 What one fetches on the market.
    prod_time number 0 Work units to manufacture one; 0 = bought only.
    size number 1 Storage space (kept for the storage rules to come).
    rounds number 1 Missiles: the rounds one missile slot holds; fitting a slot takes this many from the store (app 0.7.2).
    requires condition none Available only while this holds (as well as any topic that unlocks it).
    tech_required, tech_required2 numbers none The original's numeric tech ids, read through inheritance; with a tech_ids table and no requires, they become the prerequisite (app 0.8.0). Also on hulls and modules.

    hulls: {<id>: …} — the strategy side of a ship class. The id must be a ship class's id (hulls.comet is ships.comet); another id is a load error.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    cost number 0 Purchase price.
    prod_time number 0 Work units to manufacture one.
    upkeep number 0 Per month, per craft (also read from the original's maint_cost through inheritance).
    speed number 1000 Map units per hour on the star map.
    slots mapping kind: count none Equipment slots: {beam: 2, missile: 2, special: 1} (also read from the original's mount counts through inheritance).
    requires condition none Available only while this holds.
    components mapping component: amount none With logistics: what a main base builds one from. A hull with components is not paid for in money when its manufacture starts; it waits until the components are in the base's store (app 0.9.0).

    base_modules and base_types¶

    base_modules: {<id>: …} — what a base is built from.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Display name.
    group id the id Its limits group (research, hangar, sensor, …): a base type's limits cap modules per group.
    cost number 0 Build price, paid when ordered.
    build_time time 72 h Time to build; the module works from then on. With economy.module_time: days, a module without build_time takes its prod_time (the original's key) in days (app 0.8.0).
    maintenance number 0 Per month.
    effects mapping name: number none Campaign quantities it adds while working: research_rate, production_rate, sensor_range, hangar (craft housed), quarters (pilots housed), storage, defence, income.
    requires condition none Available only while this holds.

    As built, the engine reads production_rate (manufacturing), sensor_range (detection and revealing hidden systems) and hangar (craft a base houses). research_rate is a number the research_model.rate expression may use through module_count; quarters, storage, defence and income are accepted and summed but not yet acted on.

    base_types: {<id>: …} — kinds of base.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Display name.
    cost number 0 Build price.
    build_time time 168 h Time to build the hub and its template modules; the base is under construction until then.
    max_bases number 8 The most bases of this type.
    limits mapping group: count none The most modules of each group.
    template ids none Modules a new base comes with.
    sensor_range number 0 The hub's own sensor range, map units (modules add to it).
    hangar, quarters number 0 Craft and pilots the hub alone houses.
    max_distance number 0 A new base must be within this many map units of one of yours (0 = anywhere).

    raids¶

    raids: {<id>: …} — enemy flights the campaign launches now and then. Meeting one is a battle.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Shown on the map and in messages.
    mission id required The mission flown when it is intercepted (missions.<id>; unknown = load error).
    faction id required Who flies it (factions.<id>; unknown = load error).
    every time 72 h Time between rolls.
    chance fraction 50 % Chance per roll that a raid launches (45%).
    requires condition none Rolled only while this holds (day >= 10).
    target base / system base What it heads for: one of the player's bases, or any system.
    speed number 600 Map units per hour.

    On a free-travel map a launched raid appears well outside the map, in a random direction, and flies straight for its target; on a lane map it enters at a system and follows the lanes (Travel). See Raids and battles.

    incomes and expenses¶

    incomes: {<id>: {name, every, amount}} and expenses: {<id>: {name, every, amount}} — an amount (an expression) added or taken every period (every, default 30 days). Each payment is a line in the message log. Module maintenance, hull upkeep and pilot salary are charged monthly on their own; use expenses for anything else.

    pilots and scores¶

    pilots: — pilot rules.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    stats mapping stat: [min, max] none Each pilot's stats, rolled in the range. Any names.
    ranks mapping rank: experience none Ranks in ascending order with the experience each needs.
    salary number 0 Per month, per living pilot.
    recruit_fee number 0 Cost to hire one.
    pool number 6 Recruits offered at a time (refreshed every month).
    names list of text none Names to draw from.
    heal_days number 14 Days an injured pilot is out.
    survive fraction 60 % Chance that a pilot shot down ejects (injured) rather than dies.

    scores: {<name>: points} — mission score names. A mission outcome's score(<name>) adds the points to the campaign score and the month's score; the name undefended is used when a raid reaches its target unopposed.

    flags and economy¶

    flags: [<id>, …] — campaign flags that conditions may test with flag(x) before any mission sets them (set_flag(x) names in mission outcomes are added automatically).

    economy:

    Key Type Default Meaning
    delivery time 48 h Time for a purchase to arrive.
    salvage_per_kill number 0 Money per enemy destroyed when an outcome says salvage.
    cost_scale number 1 Multiplies every item and hull cost, sell_price, hull upkeep and module cost and maintenance (app 0.8.0). The interceptor campaign sets 1000: the original's money is in thousands.
    module_time days — Modules without build_time take their prod_time in days (app 0.8.0; the original's module times are days).

    tech_ids¶

    tech_ids: {<number>: <research id>} (app 0.8.0) — for data inherited from a game that names prerequisites by number. A topic's prereq1 – prereq3, and an item's, hull's or module's tech_required and tech_required2, are looked up in the table and become research(<id>) conditions joined with and. Rules:

    • A written requires: always wins; the numbers are used only when there is none.
    • A number the table does not list is left out of the condition (the original's ids for alien craft and installations, 75 and up, are not research topics).
    • Without a tech_ids table the numeric keys are ignored.
    • The research ids are checked like any other name: a table entry naming an unknown topic is a load error once a condition uses it.

    How a campaign plays the data¶

    • Time passes an hour at a time (smaller at the end of a jump). Each hour: construction finishes, each base researches its topic at research_model.rate, each base manufactures its queue (first job first) at its production_rate, deliveries arrive, incomes and expenses pay, pilots heal, raids roll and move, sensors detect, interceptions fly.
    • The month (every 720 hours): maintenance, upkeep and salaries are charged; the monthly report gives the month's score, the total, the costs and the funds; a new pool of recruits is offered.
    • Bases. A new base is built in a system that has none, at most max_bases of a type, within max_distance of one of yours; it comes with its template modules and is finished after build_time. A module is refused beyond its group's limit or while not available.
    • Buying pays now and delivers to the current base after economy.delivery. Selling takes one item from the current base's store at sell_price. Manufacture queues at the base; finished units go into its store (hulls become craft there). Each unit is paid for as it starts (its cost); a job waits while the money is short (app 0.9.0; until then manufacture was free). With logistics, a hull that has components is paid in components instead: it starts when they are in the base's store.
    • Interception sends every craft of the current base that is at home and has a ready pilot.
    • Fitting (app 0.7.2): fit best fills each of the craft's hull slots with the costliest item of that kind in its base's store, as many as the hull has slots of that kind (a missile slot takes the item's rounds); what was fitted before goes back into the store first. A campaign battle flies the fit: fitted beam items become the player's class's beams and fitted missile items its racks, matched to beams: and missiles: of the same id (items.blaster ↔ beams.blaster); ids with no such weapon are skipped, and with no beams fitted the class keeps its own. The refit changes only that battle's copy of the rules (wingmen of the same class fly it too).

    Travel¶

    On a travel: free map everything flies straight. On a travel: lanes map (app 0.7.3):

    • a raid enters at the system farthest from its target that has none of the player's bases, and follows the shortest way along the lanes to the system nearest its target;
    • an interception flies the lanes from its base to the system nearest the contact, then straight to the contact, and comes home along the lanes;
    • the map draws each contact's way ahead. Routes are kept in saves.

    The shortest way is by distance along the lanes (Dijkstra's algorithm, Campaign::lane_route); systems no lane joins are reached in one straight hop. Endurance and stranding are not built.

    Raids and battles¶

    1. A raid's roll succeeds: a contact starts outside the map and heads for its target.
    2. When a contact comes within a base's sensor range (hub plus sensor_range effects) it is detected — the clock stops with a message — and shows on the map.
    3. The player sends the current base's ready craft to intercept it. They fly at their hull's speed; when they reach it the clock stops and the player chooses: fly the raid's mission, with the first craft's hull as the player's ship, or break off (the contact flies on).
    4. The battle's debrief comes back to the campaign: the outcome's actions apply — score(<name>), money(<n>), set_flag(<id>), salvage (money: salvage_per_kill × kills, and the raid's id is recorded, so salvaged(<raid id>) holds from then on — fixed in app 0.9.0; before, the display name was recorded and never matched), lose(<what>) (logged). The leader is credited the kills and experience (kills + 1); every pilot in the party a mission. A killed result loses the craft, and its pilot ejects injured (survive) or dies. win and draw remove the contact; lose and retreat leave it flying. Leaving a campaign battle early from the pause menu is a retreat.
    5. A contact that reaches its target unopposed: a base target loses its newest finished module, and the undefended score applies.

    Logistics¶

    App 0.9.0, plan items 4.14 – 4.17. An optional layer of the strategy game: raw resources are mined from deposits in star systems, manufacturing centers turn them into components by recipes, and main bases build hulls from components. Nothing is shipped on speculation: demand pulls supply up the chain, and automatic government supply convoys carry each filled order along the lanes. Survey ships find out what a system holds; research stations work on topics no ordinary base can. The design is Logistics: facilities and supply lines; requirements R-STR-012 – R-STR-027.

    A campaign has logistics only if it defines at least one facility_types entry. Without one, every key below is ignored and the campaign plays as before (the interceptor campaign has none, deliberately). The definition sections (resources, components, recipes, facility_types, research_sites) layer across packs like any other definitions.

    Amounts are plain numbers in the resource's unit. Mining rate is per day; everything else in time takes the usual units (24h, 10d).

    resources and components¶

    Section Key Type Default Meaning
    resources.<id> name text the id Display name of a raw resource (mined).
    unit text empty The unit shown after amounts (t, kg).
    components.<id> name text the id Display name of a manufactured component.

    recipes¶

    recipes: {<id>: …} — what a manufacturing center makes.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    component id the recipe's id The component made. One recipe per component is used (the first).
    inputs mapping resource or component: amount none What one run consumes, taken from the center's store when the run starts.
    time time 24 h How long one run takes (at least 0.1 h).
    output number 1 How much one run makes.

    An input or output that is neither a resource nor a component is a load error.

    facility_types¶

    facility_types: {<id>: …} — what the player may build in a star system.

    Key Type Default Meaning
    name text the id Display name.
    role mining / manufacturing / research required What it does. Another value is a load error.
    cost number 0 Build price, paid when ordered.
    time time 10 d Time to build; it works from then on.
    storage number 500 Mining: the most goods its store holds; extraction stops when it is full.
    lines number 1 Manufacturing: recipes running at once.
    research_rate number 0 Research: work units an hour on its project.
    hub id none The battle object at the facility, with its construction-stage looks (read; not yet used — R-STR-022).

    Where a facility may be built (fixed rules per role, app 0.9.0; the design's placement: expressions are not built — DEV-049). At most one facility of each type per system, and:

    Role Needs
    mining A surveyed system with at least one deposit.
    research A surveyed system that meets at least one research site's features.
    manufacturing A system with one of the player's bases, or at most two lanes from one (a system no lane reaches counts as one straight hop).

    research_sites¶

    research_sites: {<id>: {features: [<tag>, …]}} — a site is any system whose features include all the listed tags. Station-only topics name the site they need (below).

    On the map, hulls and topics¶

    Where Key Type Meaning
    A map system (maps.<id>.systems.<id>) deposits mapping resource: {rate, reserve, body} What a mining facility there extracts: rate per day (default 1), reserve a number or unlimited (the default), body descriptive. An unknown resource is a load error. Only the start map's systems are read.
    features list of tags Matched against research_sites.
    A hull (hulls.<id>) components mapping component: amount What a main base builds one from (instead of money). An unknown component is a load error.
    A topic (research.<id>) lab facility type Only a facility of this type may research it; the topic is not offered to bases. Unknown = load error.
    site research site Only a station in a system meeting this site. Unknown = load error.
    start_cost mapping resource or component: amount Materials delivered once to begin; then research runs without further supply.

    logistics¶

    The supply settings (a global section).

    Key Type Default Meaning
    vessels number 3 Vessels in each supply convoy; each carries an even share of the cargo.
    ambush_chance fraction 30 % Chance, rolled when a convoy launches, that it will be attacked on its way; the point along its route is rolled then too.
    ambush_loss fraction 35 % Chance that each vessel of an ambushed convoy is lost when the ambush is auto-resolved (no ambush_mission, or the player lets it fight through).
    survey_attack_chance fraction 25 % Chance, rolled when a survey ship launches, that it is attacked on the way.
    survey_speed number 900 Survey ship speed, map units an hour.
    convoy_speed number 500 Convoy speed, map units an hour.
    ambush_mission mission id none The battle flown when the player escorts an ambushed convoy (app 0.9.2). With it, an ambushed convoy waits where it is and the clock stops: the player flies its escort, and the share of the mission's cargo: role still aboard at the end is delivered — or lets it fight through (auto-resolved as below). Without it every ambush is auto-resolved. An unknown mission is a load error. The default campaign sets convoy_ambush.

    How the chain runs¶

    Every hour, after the rest of the hour step (Architecture › Campaign):

    1. Demand at bases. Each hull job not yet started needs its hull's components. What the base's store lacks, less what is already on its way, is ordered from the nearest ready manufacturing center (nearest by straight-line distance).
    2. Mining facilities extract each deposit of their system at rate / 24 an hour, until the store is full (storage) or the reserve runs out.
    3. Manufacturing centers finish runs (the output goes into the store), then look at the orders they must fill: when the store plus the runs under way do not cover an order, a free line starts the component's recipe if its inputs are in store; if they are not, the missing inputs are ordered from the nearest mining facility whose system has a deposit of them.
    4. Research stations with a project first order its start_cost; when it is in store it is used up, and from then on the station adds research_rate an hour to the topic.
    5. Filled orders leave as convoys. When a facility's store holds an order's amount, it is taken out and a convoy of vessels vessels leaves along the lanes for the order's base or facility. The ambush roll and point are decided now.
    6. Convoys travel at convoy_speed. At the ambush point each vessel may be lost (ambush_loss) and a message says how many; on arrival the surviving share of the cargo is delivered. A short delivery is simply wanted again: the next hour's demand orders the rest.
    7. Hull jobs whose components are in the base's store start, using them up; the factory then works on them as usual.
    8. Survey ships arrive. One that was attacked is lost half the time; otherwise the system is surveyed and a message lists its deposits (with rates) and the research sites it meets.

    An order is placed only when a facility exists that can fill it: a base without a reachable manufacturing center simply waits. Facilities report when they are complete.

    The player's orders¶

    On the strategy screen's Supply tab (User guide › Campaign):

    • Survey the chosen system from the current base — it must be within that base's sensor range (hub plus sensor_range effects) and not already surveyed.
    • Build a facility of any type in the chosen system, paying its cost (the row says why not when it is not allowed).
    • Research here: on a finished research station, start a station-only topic whose lab is its type, whose site its system meets and whose requires holds.

    Research stations and station-only topics¶

    research:
  theremin_shielding:
    name: Theremin shielding
    size: 24000
    requires: research(heat_rays)
    lab: research_station        # only this facility type researches it
    site: nebula_lab             # only in a system meeting this site
    start_cost: {cosmium: 20, dilithium: 2}

    The topic's progress is shared with the rest of the tree: when it reaches size it is known, like any other topic, and its unlocks apply.

    The default campaign's logistics¶

    resources/default/strategy/logistics.yaml: four resources (iron, gold, cosmium, dilithium), three components (hull plating, rocket motors, ray emitters) with a recipe each, the three facility types (mining facility, manufacturing center, research station), two research sites (nebula_lab, pulsar_array), deposits in every one of the Reach's six systems and two features, the components of all four hulls, and two station-only topics (theremin_shielding, pulsar_navigation). Every hull of the default campaign is therefore built from components: a Guard player who wants more craft must survey, mine and manufacture first.

    Not built yet¶

    Convoy ambushes can be flown since app 0.9.2 (ambush_mission; the battle is described in Missions › The convoy ambush). Not built: facility fighter pools and escorts (R-STR-025), strikes on facilities under construction from 50 % (R-STR-023), construction-stage looks on the map and in battle (R-STR-022), captured cargo and enemy supply depots (R-STR-020), third-party convoys (R-STR-027), supply-vessel cargo capacity (R-STR-026), facility upgrades, and the design's placement: expressions (fixed rules per role instead). The manufacturing center's and research station's storage is not enforced.

    The interceptor campaign from the player's data¶

    App 0.8.0, plan item 4.12. The X-COM campaign's strategy layer is built from the player's own GameSettings.ini and Interceptor.exe; the pack holds no values from them.

    • tools/interceptor_strategy.py (Python, standard library) reads the player's files and writes packs/interceptor/strategy/references.yaml: the tech-id table (tech_ids:) and inherit: original:GameSettings.ini/<section> references for 71 research topics, 55 items and 34 X-COM base modules — section names and ids only. Run it on the player's data and review the diff:
    python3 tools/interceptor_strategy.py resources/xcom-cd/Game/ProgramFiles \
    > packs/interceptor/strategy/references.yaml
    • The tech-id table comes from the executable, which keeps it as 12-byte entries of a pointer to an R_ section name, a string id and the tech id; the tool finds the run of entries whose pointers lead to R_ names. Derived (the layout is inferred, not documented). With it, 67 of the 69 TechRequired values resolve to topics; eight Prereq links point to a topic of a higher Level; ids 75 and up (alien craft and installations) are not in it and are left out of conditions.
    • packs/interceptor/strategy/campaign.yaml is written by hand on top: a generated Frontier map, downlink research (research_model.rate from the three downlink module types, switching loses progress), hulls inheriting Ship_IntType1 – Ship_IntType3, missile rounds, module groups and effects, the X-COM base type, corporate funding, raids flying the original mission types, pilots and scores. economy.cost_scale: 1000 and economy.module_time: days adapt the original's units; start.money_from reads the starting treasury for the chosen difficulty. Every estimate is marked DERIVED in the file (DEV-050): the start, the map's size in systems, delivery, salvage, hull speeds, rounds, module groups and effects, the base type's cost and hub sensor range, funding and raids. Pilot rules and score points are authored values too.
    • Research has no authored unlocks: items, hulls and modules are gated by their own TechRequired through tech_ids.

    It has not been play-tested. A six-month --strategy-soak with the automatic player researches 4 of the 71 topics on the starting base's one downlink (the default campaign: 12 of 14), so the research pace in particular needs the developer's judgement.

    Trying a campaign out¶

    • openinterceptor --campaign <id> --strategy-soak <days> (app 0.8.0) plays the strategy layer without a window for that many days with a simple automatic player (campaign::autoplay: every base researches the first topic it can, a lab and a workshop are built when there is money, every detected contact is intercepted, battles alternate win, win, retreat) and prints one line: the date, funds, score, bases, craft, topics known, battles and messages.
    • On the strategy screen the testbed has the original's own cheats (app 0.8.1): Ctrl+M payday (a million), Ctrl+K knowitall (every topic known), Ctrl+B quickbase (every base and module under construction finished — not facilities) and Ctrl+J jump to battle from the contact chosen on the Contacts tab (User guide › Campaign).

    The default campaign as the example¶

    Perils of the Void (resources/default/strategy/) uses every section:

    • Start: 1 March 2084, 2 500 000 in funds, the Gatehouse base (guard_base) at Mercury Gate, three Comets, four pilots, rocketry known, six blasters and twelve seeker rockets.
    • Map reach: fixed, lane travel, 1 unit = 0.001 ly; six systems — Mercury Gate, Ajax, Cinder, Vesta Reach, Tycho Drift, Nebula Haven — joined by seven lanes, and The Dark Moon, a hidden black hole far to the east, found only when a base's sensors reach it.
    • Research: labs (6 + module_count(laboratory) * 12 per hour, keep_progress); twelve topics from rocketry through heat_rays, ion_rays, saucer_hulls (requires salvaged(saucer_raid) or salvaged(scout_probe)), saucer_propulsion, disintegrators, deep_radar, shipyards, thunderbolt_design, robot_brains (salvaged(robot_raid)), victory_design and regents_engine, unlocking beams, missiles, the Meteor, Thunderbolt and Victory hulls and the deep-radar and shipyard modules; and two station-only topics, theremin_shielding and pulsar_navigation (app 0.9.0, Logistics).
    • Economy: delivery 48 h, salvage 15 000 a kill; the Senate stipend every 30 days, 400000 + 60000 * bases + month_score * 200 + relation(senate) * 3000 (the standing term since app 0.10.1); cosmium sales every 7 days, 12000 * module_count(cosmium_refinery); fuel and stores 4000 * craft a week. Eight items (four beams, three missiles, cosmium), four hulls, nine module kinds (hangar, quarters, radar, deep radar, laboratory, workshop, shipyard, cosmium refinery, ray battery) and the guard_base type (template hangar, radar, laboratory; at most 6 bases, 3500 units apart).
    • Raids: Dominion scouts (intercept_scouts), saucer raids (defend_station, from day 10), colony raids (colony_defence, day 20), robot assaults (outpost_raid, day 45) and the Black Comet ambush by raiders (pocket_ambush, day 15) — each a mission of the default campaign (Missions); and since app 0.10.1 the Eclipse Engine's shipments (eclipse_shipment, chance: 0%: launched only by the plan).
    • Pilots: flying, gunnery and nerve; five ranks from cadet to commander; salary 6000, recruit fee 40 000, five recruits a month, 12 days to heal, 70 % survive; twelve mission scores.

    The unit test campaign::tests::the_default_campaign_runs_six_months plays it for six months with an automatic player: it stays solvent, researches at least four topics, fights raids and round-trips through a save. --strategy-soak 180 with the same player (app 0.8.0) reached 12 of the 14 topics — the automatic player builds no facilities, so the two station-only topics stay unknown.

    Limits in app 0.10.3¶

    • No endurance or stranding on the map; no fitting rules (fits), power draw or stock designs — fitting is best available only, and slots of kinds other than beam and missile are filled but have no effect in battle.
    • Mission outcome actions relation(…), capture, cargo_lost and cargo_captured are still ignored by the campaign (app 0.10.3): a story event's relation(…) moves a standing (Story layer), a mission's does not yet. A flown convoy escort delivers its surviving share through cargo: instead.
    • Logistics: see Not built yet — fighter pools, construction strikes, depots, third-party convoys, cargo capacity, facility upgrades and placement: expressions are not built.
    • No designs, training, bravery, dismantling, funding evaluations or the 3D base view yet.
    • Names and descriptions in the default campaign's strategy files are plain English, not text keys, so they are not yet translatable.
    • The interceptor strategy layer is built but not play-tested, and its estimates are DERIVED (above).
    • Save files record pack versions but do not compare them.
    • Fixed in app 0.9.0: manufacture is paid per unit as each starts (it was free), and salvaged(<raid id>) now holds after a salvage (it compared the display name).
