    Modding¶

    Modding is the main objective of OpenInterceptor. The engine is a general space-combat simulator toolkit; X-COM: Interceptor is one content pack for it. Everything a game is made of — ships, weapons, stations, cockpits, scenarios, missions, later the tech tree and the campaign — is data in a pack, and packs layer on top of each other.

    The most likely use is a custom campaign that uses none of the original assets: your own models, textures, sounds, rules and missions. Such a pack runs with --no-original and can be shared freely, because it contains nothing of MicroProse's.

    flowchart LR
    CD[("player's original data<br/>installed copy or CD (optional)")] --> I["packs/interceptor<br/>rules + scenarios"]
    S["resources/default<br/>campaign: default, original-free"]
    M1["mods/my-campaign<br/>(user mods folder)"]
    M2["mods/balance-tweak"]
    I --> L{{"layered ruleset<br/>later packs override earlier"}}
    S --> L
    M1 --> L
    M2 --> L
    L --> E["engine: sim, render, HUD"]

    What a mod can do¶

    Stage You can change
    1 Ships (model, cockpit, flight values, mounts, hull/armour/shields, energy), the flight model itself (newtonian or assisted) and arena size, beam weapons, missiles, stations and objects, factions, cockpits (2D art at any resolution, own design space, 3D cockpit models), instrument layout, event sounds, scenarios with any mix of objects and nav points, additional models (OBJ) and textures (PNG/TGA), per-asset replacement of any original model, texture, image or sound
    2 (in progress) Done: mod kinds (campaign, addon, library) with parent, targets, format; YAML mod files with schemas (format 2: units, did-you-mean errors, editor completion); ship systems and special equipment (systems, systems+), power sharing (power), damage to systems and repair (damage), capture (capturable), hyperspace time, crippled scenario objects (destroyed_systems, hull), the power and systems instruments; AI behaviours as state machines with the condition language; difficulty levels with per-level values and variants; 3D cockpit instruments anchored in perspective and the F12 layout editor; looks (model sets with damage textures and states, debris sets, effects); missile guidance kinds, damage modes and mines; station modules at attachment points; AI target preferences and systems use (cloak, ECM, power); the sound cue table; point defence; sensor and jamming probes; text keys and translations. To come: XSPT explosions, model-set extras
    3 (in progress) Done: missions as data — roles with class lists, counts and arrivals, waves, objectives, optional objectives, timers, outcomes (actions), radio chatter, debriefs; the tablet console's look per campaign and cockpit (console, Console themes). hazards (asteroid fields, minefields, black holes, ion storms; Missions › Hazards); environments — orbit, pockets and atmospheric battles over generated or heightmap terrain with sky, fog and ground installations (Environments and terrain); radio voices — pilot voice sets, spoken chatter and the computer voice (Radio voices). To come: terrain detail textures and scattered objects, the campaign context, Tier 2 scripts
    4 (in progress) Done (app 0.6.6 – 0.9.2): formations for wingmen; the strategy layer as data — the start, fixed and generated star maps with lane travel, research with prerequisites and unlocks, items, hulls and fitting, base types and modules with limits and effects, incomes and expenses as expressions, raids that become battles, pilots, scores, saves; the interceptor strategy layer from the player's data through tech_ids (0.8.0); logistics — resources, recipes, facilities, surveys, demand-driven supply convoys, research stations (0.9.0); flown convoy ambushes with cargo containers, tugs and escorts (ambush_mission, cargo:, escort:, waves every:, towable, cargo, the escort and travel manoeuvres) and the nebula backdrop (0.9.1 – 0.9.2); fitted shields and armour — fitted: on ship classes, item protection (0.15.0). To come: fitting rules and power draw, fighter pools, construction strikes, depots, evaluations, designs
    5 (in progress) Done (app 0.10.0 – 0.10.3): the story layer — events with choices and mission offers, arcs, rival plans built from interceptable shipments, dynamic relations that stop raids and decide who fights whom in battle, the strategist's aggression, endings and victory/defeat conditions, the encyclopaedia; both campaigns' stories. To come: rival logistics and depots, third-party convoys, allied factions in battle, relation effects on prices, the Think.ini strategist, the event debugger
    6 (in progress) Done (app 0.11.0 – 0.16.0): screen layouts for the strategy screen (screens.starmap, app 0.16.0); the ship status display instrument (ship_status, app 0.15.1); the main menu and credits roll (campaign.menu, campaign.credits, Skins › The main menu); interactive music sets with states, segments and stingers; skins — colours, a nine-slice panel and a font — for the menu and strategy screens; chapter title cards (card); the original's Smacker films (video, campaign.intro_video, an ending's video); engine cutscenes with keyframed craft, timed fire, cameras, cuts and captions (campaign.intro, play_cutscene); the cockpit comms panel with speakers' names and faces (voices name, face, chatter speaker; app 0.13.1). To come: layouts for the other screens, custom panels, X-COM talking heads mapped to speakers, the X-COM wrapper screens, video files other than Smacker, the rest of the cutscene format

    Two ways to change the game¶

    1. Rules and scenarios — YAML files in a pack. Add new ships, weapons and arenas, or change any value of an existing one by overriding just that key (Rulesets).
    2. Assets — drop a file at an original asset's override path in a pack and it replaces that asset everywhere (Overrides and extraction). openinterceptor --extract <folder> writes every original asset, already converted to OBJ/PNG/WAV, at exactly those paths: extract, edit, copy the edited file into your mod.

    The simplest useful mod is one file: a repainted cockpit at modern resolution (walkthrough). Replacing the 2D cockpit with a 3D one is a few lines of rules plus a model (Cockpits).

    Comparison with XWVM¶

    XWVM plays the original X-Wing from the player's own data, and faces the same problem: 1990s assets by default, modern content when the player wants it. Public sources (a GamingOnLinux article, a Steam discussion, changelog quotes on Mudspike, and the scotsdez/xwvm-sidecars-xwing repository; ModDB refused automated access) describe its approach:

    XWVM OpenInterceptor
    Original assets Read from the player's install; none shipped Same
    Modern content A separate HD Asset Pack download (new ship models, 3D cockpits, re-recorded music) Any pack or mod
    Switching Whole packs activated in the launcher's Game Content screen Whole packs switched off in settings.yaml (packs: {disabled: […]})
    Granularity Per pack; no documented per-asset override scheme Per asset: a file at the override path replaces one original asset; everything else still comes from the original data
    Cockpits Legacy VGA cockpit or a 3D cockpit with instruments on in-cockpit screens Original 2D cockpit, HD/widescreen 2D art with its own design space, or a 3D cockpit model with instruments anchored in perspective on its dashboard, placed with the in-game layout editor
    Missions "Sidecar" XML beside the unmodified original mission Scenario YAML files; rules inherit from original INI sections without editing them
    Tools No public extractor; community format tools Built-in --extract to OBJ/PNG/WAV at override paths

    Both keep original files untouched and never redistribute them. XWVM's lesson on pack activation (toggling a pack had no effect until a restart path was taken) is why our pack list is read once, deterministically, at start-up.
