    Format: strategy layer¶

    Status: design; implemented in Stage 4 (map, economy, research, bases, ships, pilots) and Stage 5 (relations, strategist). Files in strategy/*.yaml (YAML, the mod format of the implemented rules files). Numbers that vary by difficulty take one value per level ({per_level: [..]}, implemented); any quantity the original computes with a formula takes an expression in the condition language (arithmetic over campaign queries), so the formulas are data too.

    As built (app 0.7.0 – 0.7.3)

    The built part of this design, with every key, default and error, is on the modding page Strategy layer. Built: start (one start; DEV-046), maps (fixed and generated; free and lane travel), star_classes, research and research_model (mode, rate, switching), items (plus rounds), hulls, base_modules, base_types (limits, template, max_distance in place of a placement expression), raids (the built stand-in for squadrons and the strategist), incomes, expenses, pilots (stats, ranks by experience, salary, recruit fee, pool, healing, survival), scores, flags and economy (delivery, salvage_per_kill), all expressions in the campaign context (script::campaign). Not built yet: bodies, probes and sensors as items, factions and relations, evaluations, resources, markets and factories as sections, storage, salvage tables, capture, designs, fitting rules, training, bravery, injuries by health, strategists. Several keys are simpler than designed (a number where an expression is shown, upkeep as a plain monthly number); see Deviations DEV-046.

    As built (app 0.8.0 – 0.9.0)

    For the original's own data (app 0.8.0): tech_ids: {<number>: <topic>} translates the numeric Prereq1..3 and TechRequired/TechRequired2 read by inheritance into research(x) prerequisites; economy.cost_scale (the original's money is in thousands) and economy.module_time: days; start.money_from: original:<file>/<key> reads the starting money from the difficulty level's section. Resources and the logistics sections (app 0.9.0): resources, components, recipes, facility_types, research_sites, logistics, and deposits, features, hull components, topic lab, site, start_cost.

    Star map¶

    maps: {<id>: …}¶

    maps:
  frontier:                          # interceptor: generated like the original
    kind: generated
    size: [60000, 60000]             # map units (2D)
    unit_ly: 0.000167                # map unit → light years (so 1.5 ly ≈ 9000 units)
    systems: [30, 45]                # or a table rolled on d100
    star_classes: table:spectral     # d100 table → star class
    bodies: {count: [0, 5], slots: 5, table: bodies}
    names: text:star_names           # pool
    guarantee: "black_hole >= 1"     # the gateway
    travel: free                     # free hyperjumps, or lanes
    hidden: [black_hole]             # not drawn until found
    seed: random                     # or a number for a fixed campaign
  reach:                             # default campaign: a fixed, hand-made map
    kind: fixed
    systems:
      mercury_gate: {pos: [1200, 800], class: yellow, bodies: [planet:rocky, belt:thin]}
      ajax: {pos: [4300, 1500], class: red_dwarf, bodies: [planet:ice, belt:rich]}
    lanes:
      - {between: [mercury_gate, ajax], length: 48h}
    travel: lanes
    Construct Covers
    star_classes: {<id>: …} Visual (sun model, colour, size), d100 range, mineral richness table, hazard (black hole: hidden, swallows probes, battle gravity).
    bodies: {<id>: …} Planet, moon or asteroid field: size, richness, the backdrop model used in battles in its orbit, what can be built there (OPP, outpost).
    travel free (any point, range-limited: the original) or lanes (only along lanes: simpler, used by the default campaign).
    locations: {<kind>: …} Location kinds a mission can happen at: deep_space, system, orbit, base, outpost, opp, gateway, pocket (a separate battle space reached only by story).

    Sensors and probes¶

    Survey ships (a separate craft from supply ships; range: the launching station's radar range) that report a system's deposits and facility suitability are designed on Logistics. Battle probes (probe: {sensors, jamming}) are implemented (Stations, modules and probes).

    sensor keys on bases, ships, outposts and probes give a map range; contacts are visible inside the union of ranges. Probes are object classes with map_range, lifetime, movement (station, roam) and effects (reveal, jam: downlink, hunt).

    Factions and relations¶

    factions: {<id>: …} (extended)¶

    factions:
  corporate:
    name: "@faction.corporate"
    kind: patron                 # player, rival, patron, independent, hostile
    relation: {xcom: funding}    # relation is a per-holding funding share
    funding: {range: [0%, 95%], start: 50%}
    leaves: "funding <= 0%"      # holding lost for good
    names: text:corporations
    kind Meaning Interceptor Default campaign
    player The player X-COM Interplanetary Guard
    rival A strategic opponent with its own economy, research, plan and AI Aliens Umbral Dominion
    patron Funds the player; relation per holding or overall Corporations Solar Senate
    independent Has a relation score; can trade, help or turn hostile Arms dealer, GSC Nebula Cartel, Belt Prospectors
    hostile Attacks everyone; can be bribed or destroyed by events Kabron pirates, Cult of Sirius Black Comet raiders

    Relations are numbers (−100…100) or funding shares, changed by actions (add(relation(x), n)) and by evaluations. Conditions read them.

    Dynamic relations (optional per campaign)¶

    The original's relations are fixed except for corporate funding. A campaign may instead switch on dynamic relations: every pair of factions has a standing that drifts with what happens, and the standing decides who supports whom and who fights whom, so the player's actions decide their allies over time.

    relations:
  dynamic: true                         # false = hostility only from factions' hostile lists
  scale: [-100, 100]
  stances: {hostile: -40, wary: -10, neutral: 20, friendly: 60, allied: 100}   # upper bounds
  # Initial standings (unlisted pairs start at 0); symmetric unless written both ways
  start: {guard-cartel: -10, guard-senate: 30, cartel-senate: -50, "dominion-*": -100}

# How events move standings: who did what to whom
relation_rules:
  defended:
    on: "battle_won and ally_of_victim(asset_owner)"     # you saved their base, convoy or ship
    change: {asset_owner: {base: 15, convoy: 6, ship: 2, other: 8}}
    also: {enemies_of_asset_owner: -3}
  attacked:
    on: "destroyed_by_player or captured_by_player"
    change: {owner: {base: -40, outpost: -25, freighter: -10, fighter: -3, other: -10}}
    also: {enemies_of_owner: 4}
  refused:
    on: defence_request_refused
    change: {requester: -6}
  drift:
    every: 720h                         # a month
    change: {towards: 0, by: 2}         # old grudges fade
    Stance Effect in battle Effect in strategy
    allied Their ships fight on your side; you may call for help They share sensor data, sell at a discount, may fund you, ask you to defend them
    friendly Their ships help if attacked nearby Trade; occasional requests
    neutral Ignore you unless attacked Trade at normal prices
    wary Ignore you; return fire Refuse to trade; no requests
    hostile Attack on sight; their bases and convoys become targets Raid your convoys; may ally with your enemies

    Stance changes fire events (event:on_stance_change with the faction and old and new stances), so a campaign can react with messages, new missions or story steps. Battle hostility is taken from the stances at the moment the battle starts. The interceptor campaign sets dynamic: false to match the original, with corporate funding as its only moving relation; the default campaign sets dynamic: true.

    As built (app 0.10.0 – 0.10.3)

    relations is built for the player's standing with each faction: dynamic, start (by faction, not by pair), drift (monthly, towards 0) and stances (upper bounds; above the last, allied). Of the stance effects only hostile or not acts: a non-hostile faction's raids stop, and campaign battles make each faction hostile to the player or not by its stance. Not built: pairs between other factions, scale, relation_rules, on_stance_change events, allies in battle, trade and price effects. Keys: Story layer › relations; DEV-051.

    Evaluations: evaluations: {<id>: …}¶

    The monthly funding review is an evaluation: a period, an expression per holding, and a table mapping the result to changes.

    evaluations:
  funding:
    period: 720h                 # a month
    each: outpost_system
    value: "activity(xcom, here) + activity(xcom) / 10 - activity(alien, here) - activity(alien) / 5"
    change:                      # result bands → funding change range
      - {below: -1000, change: [-5%, -20%]}
      - {below: -500, change: [-2%, -10%]}
      - {below: -250, change: [0%, -5%]}
      - {below: 0, change: [0%, -1%]}
      - {equal: 0, change: 0}
      - {up_to: 250, change: [0%, 1%]}
      - {up_to: 500, change: [0%, 5%]}
      - {up_to: 1000, change: [0%, 10%]}
      - {above: 1000, change: [2%, 10%]}
    then:
      new_outposts: {rolls: "sum / 1000", table: {none: [1, 3], one: [4, 7], two: [8, 9], three: [10, 10]}}

    Economy¶

    Section Defines Interceptor Default
    resources Something mined, stored and sold Ore (kt) Cosmium
    incomes A recurring income: source, period, expression Outpost yield × exchange rate × 0.75 × funding Senate stipend per protected colony + cosmium sales
    expenses Recurring costs Module maintenance, hull upkeep, salaries Same, fewer
    markets Who sells what, prices, availability, and who buys Outposts sell; the arms dealer buys (and arms the aliens) Senate depot sells; the Cartel buys and sells (and may supply the Dominion)
    factories Manufacturing capacity and queue rules Outpost technicians (10 + 10/OPP), priorities 10/25/50/100% One shipyard per base
    deliveries How goods arrive Outpost freighters (100 CSU; lost with cargo) Supply rocket
    storage Units and limits CSU; cargo module 1000; full = no orders, loot jettisoned Same rule

    Logistics (default campaign, planned). Resources with deposits per system, three production facility types (mining facility, research station, manufacturing center), a demand-driven order chain (main base ← manufacturing ← mining) with time-based extraction and recipes, automatic government supply convoys on fixed lanes with the attack point chosen at launch, enemy supply depots, survey ships, site-specific research branches and facility fighter pools are designed on Logistics: facilities and supply lines. A campaign that leaves those sections out keeps the simpler deliveries model above — as interceptor does, by decision, to stay faithful to the original.

    Item economy keys go on the items themselves: cost, sell_price, prod_time (0 = bought off the shelf), size (storage), maintenance, requires.

    Research¶

    research:
  tri_titanium:
    name: "@research.tri_titanium"          # found name / researched name / text keys
    size: 18000                              # work units (the original's TQ)
    level: 1
    requires: "research(wraith_examination)"
    requires_any: []                         # alternative prerequisites
    appears: "salvage(wraith_hull) or captured(wraith)"   # how it becomes available
    unlocks: [items.tri_titanium_armor, hulls.firestar]   # the original's hard-coded yields
    encyclopaedia: "@db.tri_titanium"

research_model:
  mode: download                             # or labs
  rate: "module_count(downlink_std) * 30 + module_count(downlink_hyper) * 35"
  per_base: {topics: 1, max_downlinks: 3}
  switching: lose_progress
  jammed: {when: "probe_near_base(data_jammer)", effect: pause_one_downlink}
    • mode: download is the original (research done elsewhere, bandwidth-limited); mode: labs is the familiar X-COM model (staff in base labs) for other campaigns.
    • Discovery routes: prerequisites, salvage, capture, events (unlock(research: x)).
    • Salvage is salvage: {<id>: …}: per item type, survival chance by difficulty (the original's 13 *Goodies sections), what it yields (items, research topics, score).
    • Capture: a disabled object can be towed or held until victory; its class's capture key gives what it yields.

    Bases and modules¶

    base_types:
  xcom:
    hub: objects.xcom_hub
    max_bases: 8
    placement: "distance_to_own_base <= 1.5ly"
    cost: "(1500000 + template_cost) * 0.75"
    build_time: "slowest_module + 168h"
    dismantle: {refund: 50%, sell_stock: true, dismiss_pilots: true}
    limits: {shield: 1, sensor: 1, comms: 1, hangar: 3, downlink: 3, weapon: 10}
    templates:
      new: [beam_defence, beam_defence, downlink, shield, sensor]   # best available of each

    Production facilities (facility_types: mining, research, manufacturing) extend base types with production roles; see Logistics.

    Modules are object classes (objects) with effects: named campaign quantities they add while working (hangar_capacity +3, pilot_capacity +5, heal_rate x2, storage +1000, research_rate +30, base_shield 2000, sensor_range 40000, orders_in_flight, probe_launch, repair_rate x2, ecm 1km), and on_destroyed actions (lose(ships in hangar), kill(pilots in module), lose(stock)). A destroyed module stays as a wreck until repaired or dismantled.

    Ships and equipment¶

    hulls:
  firestar:                    # strategy data for a ship class (same id as ships.firestar)
    slots: {beam: 4, missile: 3, special: 4, armor: 1, engine: 1, sensor: 1, shield: 1}
    cost: 550000
    prod_time: 18000
    upkeep: {amount: 18000, per: month}
    requires: "research(tri_titanium)"

items:
  graviton_pulse:
    kind: engine               # beam, launcher, missile, armor, shield, engine, sensor, special, probe, mine
    special: {power: 300, endurance: 48h}
    cost: 185000
    requires: "research(graviton_pulse)"

designs:
  lightning_stock:             # named configuration
    hull: lightning
    fit: {beam: [laser_cannon, laser_cannon], missile: [xwinder_launcher, xwinder_launcher], engine: [conversion_drive]}
    auto: best                 # stock designs upgrade to the best available item
    price: "hull + 0.9 * parts"

    Loadout rules (fits on items: which slots, minimum sensor for a missile, power draw) are item keys; the Equip Ship screen enforces them.

    Pilots¶

    pilots:
  stats:
    intelligence: [50, 100]
    accuracy: [25, 100]
    flying: [25, 100]
    aggression: [25, 100]
    bravery: [25, 100]
    psionics: [1, 100]
    stamina: [50, 100]
    health: [6, 15]
  ranks: {rookie: 40%, ensign: 20%, lieutenant: 20%, lt_commander: 15%, commander: 5%}
  rank_up: {experience: [2, 10, 24, 38]}   # thresholds (experienced); other levels via variants
  experience: "kills / missions + missions"
  kill_value: {fighter: 1, freighter: 1, outpost: 10, opp: 5, probe: 5, mine: 0.25}
  salary: "(48000 + modifiers) / 12"
  training: {stats: [accuracy, flying, psionics], per_month: "ceil(0.1 * stat * intelligence / 100)"}
  injury:
    - {health: 5, accuracy: 50%, flying: 50%}
    - {health: 2, accuracy: 25%, flying: 25%}
    - {health: 0, state: unconscious}
    - {below: 0, state: dead}
  bravery: {kill: 5, outpost: 10, base: 25, own_base_lost: -25, flee_below: 10, flee_chance: 50%, flee_check: 30s}
  recruit: {pool: 10, refresh: monthly, fee: "10000 + 24000 + modifiers"}
  names: text:pilot_names
  voices: [m1, m2, m3, m4, m5, f1, f2, f3, f4, f5]

    Every formula is an expression, so another campaign can use completely different pilot rules (or leave out the pilots section and have none).

    Rival strategy: strategists: {<id>: …}¶

    The rival is driven by a strategist: budgets, priorities and an offence ladder, with conditions deciding when it expands or attacks. It replaces the original's hard-coded strategic AI and reads its Think.ini numbers.

    strategists:
  alien:
    faction: alien
    income: 100%                                   # of outpost yield
    budget: {plan: 50%, forces: 35%, research: 15%}   # Think.ini ResearchDefault/GoodsDefault/DoomsdayDefault
    period: 360h                                   # 15 days
    aggression:
      start: think:StartingAggression
      up_when: player_passive
      down_when: player_winning
    explore: [probes, patrols]
    expand: {new_base_when: "player_aggressive or lost_base or plan_starved or outposts_exposed"}
    outposts: {monthly_chance: "5% + day / 30 * 1%", max: 16}
    offence: [attack_probe, attack_freighter, attack_opp, attack_outpost, cripple_base,
              destroy_base, decoy_strike]          # ordered by aggression
    defence: [outposts, bases, opps, freighters]   # priority order
    tech: {levels: 3, cost: [800000, 4000000], funded_by: "module_count(data_receptor) * 20000"}   # per week
    cheats: [knows_player_start_area, instant_equipment, outposts_rebuilt]
    build_priorities: think:module_priorities      # the 31 module sections of Think.ini

    The strategist launches squadrons on flight plans; when squadrons and player assets meet, a mission is generated.

    As built (app 0.10.0)

    Only the aggression is built, as a global strategist: section (aggression: {start, min, max, up, down}, calm_after_wins) rather than strategists.<id>: it multiplies every raid's chance, rising each month the player wins fewer than calm_after_wins battles and falling otherwise. Budgets, exploration, expansion, outposts, the offence ladder, defence, tech levels, cheats, build priorities and the Think.ini values are not built. Story layer › strategist; DEV-051.
