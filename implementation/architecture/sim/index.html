    Simulation¶

    src/sim/: the battle as plain data, advanced by exactly TICK = 1/60 s per World::step. No rendering, no input devices, no clock reads, no macroquad — only glam maths and the Ruleset. That makes it deterministic and unit-testable (80+ tests). Plan: Stage 1 (flight, weapons, basic AI); Stage 2 (in progress) adds ship systems, power, damage to systems, special equipment, hyperspace and wingman orders (sim::systems), and will deepen the AI. Stage 3 adds battle hazards (sim::hazards, app 0.5.1) and the battle environment — atmosphere, ground and ceiling (sim::env, app 0.6.0; ground installations 0.6.1).

    Architecture Overview¶

    One tick: World::step()¶

    flowchart TD
    A["events.clear()"] --> B["ai::think<br/>AI pilots write Controls (two-phase)"]
    B --> B2["systems::handle_requests<br/>power settings, shield balance,<br/>tractor / cloak / ECM switches, hyperdrive"]
    B2 --> C["match speed resolved<br/>(sets Ship::speed_hold to the target's speed)"]
    C --> AIR["env::air for every live ship<br/>drag, gravity, lift (atmosphere only)"]
    AIR --> D["flight::step for every live ship<br/>throttle, turn rates, rotation, thrust/velocity, position<br/>(scaled by engine / manoeuvring / VCT efficiency)"]
    D --> D2["flight::hold_in_arena<br/>halt ships at the arena radius ('ARENA BOUNDARY')<br/>(env.arena overrides flight.arena_radius)"]
    D2 --> GC["env::ground_and_ceiling<br/>landing / crash on the terrain, ceiling exit"]
    GC --> HZ["hazards::step<br/>black-hole pull, swallowed craft destroyed"]
    HZ --> E["combat::step<br/>lock → beams → missile controls → link mode<br/>→ point_defence → move_bolts → guidance::step"]
    E --> F["combat::ship_collisions"]
    F --> S["systems::step<br/>recharge → repair → disabled check<br/>→ tractors → countermeasures → hyperdrives"]
    S --> WR["destroyed modules with a destroyed look → wrecks"]
    WR --> MO["update_modules<br/>modules follow hubs, cascade, shield generators"]
    MO --> EX["expiring ships (doppelgangers) counted down"]
    EX --> G["clear one-shot controls and requests,<br/>since_hit += TICK, time += TICK"]

    In short: AI → requests → air → flight → ground and ceiling → hazards → combat (incl. missile guidance) → collisions → systems → modules. The atmosphere's forces act before flight::step so the flight model integrates them with thrust in the same tick; the ground is checked after the move, so a craft never ends a tick below it. Requests run before flight so a switch pressed this tick (a cloak, a power change) already counts; systems run last so that damage done this tick can knock a system out before repair and recharge look at it.

    The player and the AI produce the same Controls struct and fly the same flight model.

    Flight model (sim::flight)¶

    Two models, chosen per ruleset with flight.model:

    flowchart TD
    C["Controls (player or AI)"] --> T["update_throttle<br/>set / delta / reverse;<br/>any manual input clears speed_hold"]
    T --> R["turn(): desired rates × turn_factor,<br/>exponential smoothing, quaternion update"]
    R --> M{"flight.model"}
    M -- newtonian --> N["brake? approach_vec(v, 0)<br/>hold? approach_vec(v, nose × hold)<br/>else thrust = nose × acceleration × throttle<br/>(reverse: −nose; afterburner: ×2)<br/>+ VCT thrust; cap at max_speed (+boost)"]
    M -- assisted --> A["forward speed → throttle × max_speed;<br/>drift damped; brake = 0"]
    N --> P["position += velocity × dt"]
    A --> P

    newtonian (default). Space has no drag.

    • Throttle is thrust: acceleration × throttle along the nose. At zero throttle the ship coasts at its current velocity — it does not slow down.
    • Reverse (Z) points the thrust backwards along the nose.
    • Brake (hold B, gamepad B) retro-thrusts against the velocity at full acceleration and stops exactly at zero (approach_vec).
    • Speed limit: max_speed, or max_speed + boost while the afterburner burns (double thrust, cost energy per second). Thrust may not push past the cap; excess speed (after the burner stops) bleeds off at acceleration per second.
    • Turning changes the nose, not the velocity: a hard turn at speed makes the ship slide. VCT hulls add sideways/vertical thrust at 0.5 × acceleration (VCT_THRUST_FRACTION).
    • Flight computer (speed hold): Controls::hold_speed (set every tick by AI pilots) or Ship::speed_hold (set by match speed, M) drives the velocity towards nose × hold within the hull's acceleration — holding a forward speed and cancelling drift. Any manual throttle, reverse or brake input clears speed_hold. AI pilots therefore obey exactly the same thrust limits as the player.

    assisted (the previous arcade model, kept for mods that want it): throttle sets a target forward speed (throttle × max_speed, reverse −½), the ship accelerates or decelerates towards it, sideways drift is damped, VCT slides at 25 % of max_speed (VCT_SPEED_FRACTION), brake targets zero.

    Both models: pitch and yaw rates scale with turn_factor(speed, lock_speed) = clamp(1 − |speed| / lock_speed, 0, 1); roll is not reduced. Angular velocity approaches the desired value with blend = 1 − exp(−dt / τ), τ = damping_time(angular_damp) = 0.05 + 0.06 × angular_damp. Orientation is a quaternion (rotation × from_scaled_axis(ω·dt)); body axes x = pitch (+ nose up), −y = yaw right, −z = roll right. Static objects (kind: station, mines) never translate but may spin. Recharge of energy and shields moved to systems::recharge in Stage 2 (it now depends on the power settings).

    System efficiencies. Thrust and the afterburner scale with efficiency(Engines) (no afterburner at 0), turn rates with efficiency(Manoeuvring), VCT slides with efficiency(Vct). A craft whose list lacks engines, manoeuvring or sensors is treated as having working ones that are not modelled; any other missing system does not work.

    Arena edge. After flight, hold_in_arena(ship, arena_radius) places any ship beyond the radius back on the boundary sphere and removes its outward velocity (tangential motion is kept), emitting Message "ARENA BOUNDARY" when it first arrives. arena_radius: 0 means no limit. Default 60 000 m.

    Combat (sim::combat)¶

    • Lock: the target inside the cone flight.lock_angle and within missile speed × duration for the selected missile's lock_time seconds → locked, SimEvent::LockAcquired. Leaving the cone resets it. Lock builds at efficiency(Sensors) speed, halved again while the target's ECM is on.
    • Tractor and cloak: while the tractor beam is on, beams do not fire ("BEAMS OFF-LINE: TRACTOR BEAM ON"); firing beams or a missile drops an active cloak ("CLOAK DROPPED").
    • Beams: LinkMode::Linked fires mounts in pairs, Full all at once, Unlinked one at a time; each mount has a cooldown of 1 / rate_of_fire; energy is checked for the whole group (NoEnergy event and a 0.5 s hold if short). Life = range / speed.
    • Bolt launch (app 0.3.1, R-CMB-021). A bolt starts at its muzzle, pos + r with r = rotation × mount_offset, and leaves with exactly the muzzle's velocity plus its own muzzle speed along the gun: velocity = ship.velocity + ω × r + dir × beam.speed. ω is the ship's turn rate in world axes; Ship::angular_velocity is kept in body axes, so ω = rotation × angular_velocity. A gun on the right wing of a ship yawing left is swinging forwards, and its shot keeps that speed. After launch the bolt flies straight, whatever the ship does (flight.bolt_frame: world, the default).
    • bolt_frame: ship (the arcade option): the bolt also stores its offset and velocity in the owner's body axes (local_offset, local_velocity, made with rotation.conjugate()) and a carry time from flight.bolt_carry. While carry > 0 and the owner lives, move_bolts places it from the owner each tick (owner.position + owner.rotation × local_offset), so it turns with the nose; its velocity is set to the move it makes, so the hit test sweeps the real path. When the carry runs out it keeps that last velocity and flies straight.
    • Body velocity. Ship::body_velocity() is the velocity in the ship's own axes, rotation.conjugate() × velocity, with z negated so forward reads positive: x sideways drift (+ right), y vertical drift (+ up), z forward speed. The HUD's drift readout shows it (Cockpit and HUD).
    • Bolts and missiles test the segment travelled this tick against every other ship's collision::Shape (sphere broad phase, triangle mesh for stations) so fast shots cannot tunnel.
    • Missiles are launched and flown by sim::guidance (below); impact damage on a hit; the proximity fuse detonates on a near miss (inside proximity_radius and moving away); expired missiles pop harmlessly.
    • Damage (apply_damage): fore or aft shield (by which side was hit) → armour → hull; hull ≤ 0 → alive = false, Destroyed, and every ship targeting or towing it lets go (forget_ship). Damage that gets past the armour without destroying the ship also strikes a system (systems::damage_system). A shield facing reaching zero says "FORE/AFT SHIELD DOWN".
    • Collisions: fighter–fighter spheres (60 % of radius) push apart and swap half their velocities; a fighter sweeping into a station mesh is backed out and bounced. Damage = relative speed × 0.1.

    Point defence (Stage 2)¶

    combat::point_defence runs before the missiles move: each ship with a PointDefence (range, rate, chance, energy) whose gun is cooled down and that has the energy fires at the nearest hostile missile within range (decoys included); chance is rolled on the world's seeded generator; a hit removes the missile. Each shot costs energy, sets Ship::pd_cooldown = 1 / rate and emits SimEvent::PointDefence { ship, from, to, hit } (tracer and sound).

    Probes and radar (Stage 2)¶

    systems::on_radar(world, observer, other) decides what a radar shows: detectable (a cloak hides a ship of another faction beyond its range), then within flight.radar_range — halved while systems::jammed(observer), i.e. inside the jamming radius of a live hostile probe — or within the sensors range of any live probe of the observer's faction. Objects that never move always show. Missile lock also builds at half speed while jammed. Probes are ordinary objects with a ProbeDef { sensors, jamming }.

    Since app 0.5.1 an ion storm multiplies the radar range by its sensors factor (after the jamming halving) and halves missile-lock speed again. Scenery (Ship::scenery, set on asteroids placed by a hazard) is the exception to "objects that never move always show": it shows only within range, so an asteroid field does not flood the scopes.

    Hazards (sim::hazards, app 0.5.1)¶

    The definitions come from a mission's or scenario's hazards: (content::hazards::Hazards: Scatter for asteroids and mines, BlackHole, the ion storm's factor; modder's guide). World::apply_hazards runs last when a battle is built — after every scenario object or mission role — and does two things:

    • Asteroid fields and minefields are ordinary objects, spawned once by World::scatter, so collisions, shots, damage and destruction need no new code. Points are uniform in the ball: a random direction times radius × u^(1/3) (volume grows with r³, so a plain u would crowd the centre). A point within CLEARANCE = 300 m of a live craft is rejected; at most 20 × count tries are made, so a crowded sphere places fewer rather than looping. Each gets a random orientation and an angular velocity up to spin °/s. Everything uses the world's seeded random(), so a battle is the same every time.
    • Field hazards (the black hole and the ion storm) are stored as ActiveHazards on World::hazards and act every tick. hazards::step adds black_hole_accel to every live, non-static craft and to every missile, then destroys any craft inside the hole's radius (shields and armour zeroed, then combat::apply_damage with an overwhelming amount, so the usual Destroyed event, explosion and mission bookkeeping follow). The ion storm acts through systems::on_radar and the lock code (above).

    black_hole_accel(b, p) = pull × (1000 / d)² towards the hole, capped at PULL_CAP = 20 × pull — without the cap the 1/d² term would fling a craft very near the centre across the map in one tick. The flight screen announces an ion storm as the battle starts (ION STORM: SENSORS AT n%), and the renderer draws the hole (Rendering).

    The battle environment (sim::env, app 0.6.0)¶

    World::env: Env holds the environment the battle runs in: id, physics (Space or Atmosphere), gravity, air_density, ceiling, ground (an Arc<Heightfield>, shared with the rules rather than copied per battle) and arena. World::set_environment(id) copies it from Ruleset::environments and finds the terrain's heightfield; it runs first, right after World::new in World::from_scenario and before the roles in MissionRun::start, so that ground installations placed afterwards can be set on the ground. Without an environment: the default Env is open space and nothing below applies.

    Step Function Rule
    Drag env::air v −= v · min(k·ρ·|v|·dt, 1), k = acceleration / atmosphere_max_speed²: full thrust balances drag at atmosphere_max_speed when ρ = 1. The min keeps one tick from reversing the velocity.
    Gravity env::air v.y −= g·dt, unless the class has hover.
    Lift env::air v += up · min(g·ρ·(forward/lift)², 2g) · dt for classes with lift > 0; forward is the velocity along the nose (never negative).
    Ground env::ground_and_ceiling Below ground_height + clearance (the shape radius × BODY_RADIUS_FRACTION) the craft is put back on the ground. Speed into the ground (along the terrain's normal) ≤ LANDING_SPEED = 25 m/s with up · n > 0.7 lands (the into-ground motion removed, ×0.97 slide friction); otherwise a crash: a bounce (+n × into × 1.3, then ×0.5), a Collision event and apply_damage(into² × 0.5).
    Ceiling env::ground_and_ceiling Above ceiling: alive = false, departed = true, Departed and the message LEFT THE ATMOSPHERE — the same path as a hyperspace jump, so missions count it as leaving.

    Static classes (max_speed 0) feel none of it. A craft in Simple physics feels the forces, but flight::step then sets its velocity along the nose as usual. Ground installations (ShipKind::Ground, app 0.6.1) are static (is_static); World::spawn replaces the y of such an object with ground_height + 0.25 × shape radius when the battle has a ground.

    flowchart LR
    R["Ruleset::environments / terrains<br/>(Heightfield built once)"] --> SE["World::set_environment(id)<br/>first, before placement"]
    SE --> ENV["World::env<br/>physics, g, ρ, ceiling, ground, arena"]
    ENV --> AIR["env::air (each tick)"]
    ENV --> GC["env::ground_and_ceiling (each tick)"]
    ENV --> SP["World::spawn<br/>kind: ground set down"]
    ENV --> AG["ai::avoid_ground"]
    ENV --> HUD["hud: ALT / VS, horizon"]
    ENV --> RN["render: terrain mesh, sky, fog"]

    Missile guidance (sim::guidance, Stage 2)¶

    combat fires a missile through guidance::launch, which applies the class's launch-time behaviour; guidance::step (called by combat::step every tick) steers, moves and detonates every missile, collecting blasts first and applying them after the loop (the same two-phase pattern as the AI).

    flowchart TD
    L["guidance::launch(missile)"] --> CW{"guidance"}
    CW -- cloaked_wait --> D1["slow to 20 %, life × 4"]
    CW -- parallax --> D2["release decoys: harmless missiles<br/>flying away from the launcher"]
    CW -- doppelganger --> D3["spawn a fragile copy of the launcher<br/>(expires); enemies may switch to it"]
    S["guidance::step"] --> LU["lure: a missile homing on a decoy's launcher,<br/>within LURE_RANGE (2.5 km), switches to the decoy<br/>at LURE_CHANCE (0.8) per second"]
    LU --> ST["steer: homing / sidestep (2 × turn inside 600 m, weave)<br/>/ cluster (split near the target or at half fuel)<br/>/ cloaked_wait (sleep until prey in trigger_range)<br/>/ dumbfire"]
    ST --> HIT["segment test against ships → impact;<br/>proximity fuse; fuel out"]
    HIT --> BL["blasts → apply_damage_mode<br/>(normal / bypass_shields / enveloping)"]

    area_blast(world, at, radius, damage, except) damages every ship in radius, falling off linearly, enveloping; mines use it (systems::handle_requests on a detonate request: radius = 4 × the warhead's proximity_radius, at least 100 m). The modder's view: Missiles and mines.

    Station modules (Stage 2)¶

    World::spawn_modules(hub, depth) (called by from_scenario for every placed object) spawns each modules entry of the hub's class at its attachment point — an OBJ slot_<name> or an original manipulator index, looked up in the class's collision::Shape::mounts — turned so the module's −Z follows the slot's facing, with the hub's faction and Ship::mount = (hub, offset, rotation); modules with a behaviour fly it (turret), the rest are idle; up to five levels. update_modules (every tick, after systems) puts each module back on its hub, destroys the modules of a destroyed hub, removes wrecks whose hub is gone, and zeroes a station's shields while none of its shield_generator modules survives ("STATION SHIELDS DOWN"). A destroyed module whose model set has a destroyed state becomes a Wreck { class, hub, offset, rotation } in World::wrecks, drawn by the renderer on its hub. Modder's view: Stations and modules.

    Modules on moving hubs (app 0.9.2). Any ship class may carry modules — the default campaign's cargo clipper has twelve cargo pods and a point-defence turret — and three fixes make a moving hub work, each found while building the convoy ambush:

    • No collisions within one craft. combat::ship_collisions skips a pair when one is mounted on the other or both are mounted on the same hub (Ship::mount.0). Before, the pods' overlapping hulls collided with the clipper every tick and braked it.
    • Modules jump with their hub. systems::depart departs, recursively, every live module still mounted on the ship that leaves, so a convoy that jumps takes its cargo with it (departed, not destroyed — mission::cargo_share counts it as delivered).
    • Avoidance ignores its own modules. The AI's collision avoidance skips objects mounted on itself.
    • Torn off by a tractor. A towable class (a cargo container) may be tractored even when not disabled; in systems::tractors a pull on a mounted one takes its mount (Option::take), so it is no longer placed back on the hub, and emits "CARGO CONTAINER TORN AWAY". The tug that jumps holding it captures it like a disabled ship.

    sim::tests::cargo_modules_count_until_torn_off_destroyed_or_jumped_away_with_the_hub and a_tug_tears_a_container_off_and_prefers_a_disabled_ship cover these.

    Systems (sim::systems, Stage 2)¶

    Everything a pilot manages inside the ship. Data comes from the systems, power and damage sections (Rulesets); each ship has power: Power, systems: Vec<SystemState> (one per fitted system), repairing, towing, hyperspace, disabled and departed.

    flowchart TD
    subgraph req["handle_requests (early in the tick)"]
      R1["F9/F10: cycle() a setting, wrapping E → A"]
      R2["; and ': raise() one setting,<br/>lowering the other to stay within Budget"]
      R3["S: balance Equal → Forward → Aft"]
      R4["tractor / cloak / ECM: toggle()<br/>needs fitting, condition > 0, free steps;<br/>tractor also a target in Range"]
      R5["H: start countdown (hyperspace_time / efficiency)<br/>or abort"]
    end
    subgraph step["step (after combat)"]
      S1["recharge(): energy × beam_recharge[level];<br/>shields × shield_recharge[level] × efficiency;<br/>excess over a facing's cap flows to the other"]
      S2["repair(): one system, worst first,<br/>repair_rate × damage-control multiplier"]
      S3["disabled = engines and manoeuvring both at 0"]
      S4["tractors(): collect Tow::Pull / Tow::Release, then apply"]
      S5["countermeasures(): sensors lose cloaked targets;<br/>missiles may lose cloaked / jamming targets"]
      S6["hyperdrives(): count down, 'JUMP IN n',<br/>depart(); a held disabled capturable ship is Captured"]
      S1 --> S2 --> S3 --> S4 --> S5 --> S6
    end

    Conditions. SystemState::condition() is Ok above half its hit points (efficiency 1), Damaged at half or below (0.5), Inoperative once its hit points reach 0 (0) and Destroyed below −destroy_below × max (0, for the battle). SystemState::set_hp keeps an offline flag: a knocked-out system stays Inoperative until repaired back to ONLINE_AT = 25 % of its hit points, so the first moments of repair do not bring a wreck straight back. A knocked-out switchable system switches off; a knocked-out hyperdrive aborts a countdown.

    Damage. damage_system strikes one random non-destroyed system with the damage that got past the armour × system_share, and emits SystemChanged when the condition changes.

    Repair. repair keeps working on the current job; otherwise it takes the worst-off system (first in fitting order on a tie). Speed = class repair_rate × (1 + (repair_rate of the damage control computer − 1) × its efficiency); the computer does not speed up its own repair.

    Power. raise and cycle work on a plain Power value and the PowerRules, not on the world, so they are tested exhaustively. shield_caps(shields, balance, shift) gives the fore and aft capacities shields × (1 ± shift).

    Tractor. The beam holds its target on its current bearing at a distance between 1.2 × the two radii and 0.6 × range, steering the target's velocity towards the captor's by at most strength × efficiency m/s². The hold is lost beyond 1.5 × range, if the target goes, or if the tractor is knocked out ("TRACTOR BEAM RELEASED"). Because a pull changes another ship, decisions are collected as Tow values first and applied afterwards (the two-phase pattern of ai::think).

    Cloak and ECM. detectable(world, observer, other) hides a cloaked ship of another faction beyond its cloak's range; targeting (targeting::cycle, nearest enemy) only offers detectable ships and a sensor that loses its target says "TARGET LOST". Each second, a missile homing on a ship loses its target with chance strength / 100 × efficiency per active cloak, plus per active ECM within range of the missile.

    Hyperspace. The countdown announces the last five seconds; at zero depart removes the ship (alive = false, departed = true, Departed event), and its mounted modules with it (app 0.9.2). If its tractor holds a disabled, capturable ship — or anything towable — that ship departs too and Captured { captor, prize } is emitted.

    AI (sim::ai)¶

    think reads the whole world into a list of decisions, then writes them (two-phase update). Each AI ship has a role from its scenario (ai::Behaviour): Idle, Target (slow circle, never fires), Wingman (formation and orders, below) or Enemy. A disabled AI ship does nothing but drift.

    Enemies fly a behaviour from the rules (content::behaviour, modder's guide): the scenario object's behaviour, else the class's, else the engine's builtin_attack.

    flowchart TD
    T["think: for each Enemy"] --> B["behaviour_of: object → class → builtin_attack"]
    B --> RB["run_behaviour"]
    RB --> CK{"check_clock ≤ 0?<br/>(4 × per second)"}
    CK -->|yes| TR["first transition with matching from,<br/>a different to, and when.test(ObjectContext)"]
    TR --> ST["state, state_time = 0"]
    CK -->|no| FL
    ST --> FL["fly(manoeuvre, params, skill)"]
    FL --> AT["attack / turret: aim_point<br/>(reaction lag, aim error ≤ (1 − aim) × 8°),<br/>in_burst (burst/pause), fire_cone"]
    FL --> OT["idle, circle, patrol, evade,<br/>retreat (jump), tow (tractor, haul, jump),<br/>escort (formation or attack), travel (run, jump)"]
    AT --> C["Controls"]
    OT --> C
    • Aim. Every skill.reaction seconds (aim_point) the pilot takes a fresh look at its target (position and velocity) and draws a new aim error, uniform over a disc of radius (1 − aim) × MAX_AIM_ERROR (8°). Between looks it assumes the target flies straight on from the last sighting. The lead point uses the target's velocity relative to the shooter (bolts carry the shooter's velocity).
    • Fire discipline. in_burst alternates burst seconds of firing with pause seconds of holding; guns fire only inside fire_cone and inside range (and 95 % of the first beam's reach). Missiles every missiles seconds when locked (0 = never).
    • Systems use. At each transition check, systems: conditions of the behaviour are evaluated (ObjectContext) and turned into ordinary Requests: cloak / ecm switched to match their condition, power_to_shields while shields_first holds and shields are below the top level, else power_to_beams while beams_first holds. The requests go through systems::handle_requests like the player's keys, and reach the ship once (they are cleared after the tick).
    • Missiles (Stage 3). An attacker launches only within 2.5 × its attack range (at least 2 km); its first launch comes 3 s plus a random part of one interval into the attack, and each interval varies by ±25 %. Every AI pilot's random sequence is seeded per ship in World::spawn (from its slot and the world's generator), so pilots that start together do not fire together.
    • Target choice. attack and turret keep the current target while it lives and stays visible; otherwise they pick targeting::preferred_hostile with the behaviour's prefer weights (a matching hostile counts as distance / weight; several matches multiply), within 8 km (attack) or range (turret).
    • Mines. The mine manoeuvre sets Requests::detonate when a hostile is within range.
    • Turrets. Controls::aim (a world direction) lets a ship with turret_arc > 0 fire its beams along the aim instead of the nose, if the aim is within the arc (combat::fire_beams); the turret manoeuvre stays put and sets it.
    • Tow. The tow manoeuvre finds a prize (nearest_prize): a disabled, capturable hostile first, else the nearest towable hostile, skipping anything another ship is already towing. It closes in, requests the tractor within 90 % of its range, then hauls the prize away; it remembers where it took it (AiState::anchor) and requests the hyperdrive once it is escape (default 2.5 km) from there (app 0.9.2).
    • Escort (app 0.9.2). An escort's AiState::leader and offset are set when it spawns, from the mission role's escort: (the newest live member of that role) and its formation slot. escort flies to the slot with fly_to_slot — the formation code wingmen use, split out of the wingman manoeuvre so both share it — and attacks the nearest hostile within range of the leader or of itself; with no leader left it just fights.
    • Travel (app 0.9.2). Holds speed × top speed straight on and requests the hyperdrive once it is range from where the state began (AiState::anchor again): a supply ship making for its jump point.
    • Each pilot has its own seeded random stream (AiState::rng), so random(p) and aim errors are deterministic.
    • Escapes (Stage 3). Whatever its behaviour wants, every AI pilot except Idle objects first checks two overrides, in this order: escape_black_hole — inside 4 × the hole's radius, or inside 10 × while its velocity points towards the hole, it steers directly away, holds its top speed and burns the afterburner while energy is above 20 %; then avoid_ground — below 150 m above the terrain, or when altitude + 3 s × vertical speed is below 100 m, it steers for a point 800 m ahead and 600 m up and holds 1.4 × lift (clamped between half and all of its top speed), a speed its wings can climb at. Either returns None when clear, and the behaviour's own controls are used. Since the black-hole escape, no AI craft falls in during the soaks.
    • Collision avoidance (app 0.6.7, from the user's playtest). Third in the override chain, avoid_collision(world, id) runs for every moving AI pilot (static objects and turrets never avoid; they cannot move out of the way and must keep aiming). For each other object it takes the relative position r and velocity v and finds the time of closest approach t* = −(r·v)/|v|²; objects that cannot reach it within the horizon are skipped early. The horizon is AVOID_HORIZON = 2.5 s, but 1 s for static objects and for the pilot's own target — those are attacked head-on, and a firing pass must not be broken off early. Danger is a miss |r + v·t*| under 1.5 × the combined radii + 10 m (static objects: the combined radii
    • 30 m, so attack runs on a station still get close), or something already inside twice the combined radii and closing. Against the soonest danger the pilot steers away from where the other will be at closest approach (a dead-on course breaks to its own upper right), mostly sideways, and eases off to the other's speed if it is ahead. A leader ignores its own wingmen (they keep clear of it; swerving from them as they form up, it would never fly straight). The fire buttons are kept, so avoiding does not stop a pilot shooting. Before, only wingmen avoided, and only when something was already inside twice the combined radii; the user saw wingmen and enemies ram. Test: collision_avoidance_predicts_the_closest_approach.
    • Formations (MD1, app 0.6.6). A wingman's AiState::offset is its slot in the leader's frame. MissionRun gives wingmen successive slots of their role's formation (Ruleset::formation(role.formation), else flight.formation; FormationDef::slot(k)), counting the wingmen of every role already spawned. ai::next_formation(world, leader) (the Shift+O order) takes the formation after World::wing_formation in rules.formations (wrapping), gives each of the leader's living wingmen the next slot in ship order and orders them to form up; it returns the formation's id, or None with no formations or no wingmen. Slots are still pushed out to flight.formation_gap by formation_slot.

    Wingmen follow WingOrders from their leader (order_wingmen); they fend for themselves if the leader dies. When they engage they fly attack with the parameters and skill of their class's behaviour (its first attack state):

    Order Behaviour
    Formation (default; "ignore my target") Hold the formation slot.
    AttackMyTarget Attack the leader's current target if it is hostile.
    FireAtWill Engage the nearest hostile near the leader.
    Evasive Break formation and weave near the leader for EVASIVE_TIME = 10 s, then form up.
    Retreat Run from the nearest hostile at full speed (afterburner while energy lasts) and start the hyperdrive.

    wing_status(world, leader) returns one line per wingman: name, hull and shield percentages, current order, and DISABLED if so.

    Debugging¶

    • To compare the two flight models, set flight: {model: assisted} in a mod's rules (or a small addon to the default campaign) and fly the same scenario; the HUD status line shows BRAKE while braking and HOLD n while a speed hold is active.

    • Because step is deterministic, a bug can be reproduced as a unit test: build a world with sim::test_support::world() (a tiny in-memory ruleset), place ships, set controls, call step in a loop, assert. Most tests in sim/ are written exactly this way.

    • --view chase or --view chase-manual shows the player's own ship to check orientation and engine glow; the MFD Status page shows shields, armour, hull and energy live, and the Systems page each system's condition.
    • The systems-trial scenario (both campaigns) exercises power, tractor tow and capture, cloak and hyperspace. Scenario objects with destroyed_systems = engines, manoeuvring start disabled, and hull = 0.3 starts them damaged, so a system state can be set up without fighting for it.
    • Every power or equipment refusal is a pilot message ("INSUFFICIENT POWER FOR …", "NO TARGET IN TRACTOR RANGE", "… INOPERATIVE"); the message line and L (repeat) show why a switch did nothing.
    • An enemy that "does nothing": check its role (ai: enemy in the scenario) and that its behaviour's states can act (an attack state with a range its beams reach). --check reports behaviours whose conditions do not parse.
    • Balance by numbers: --soak <scenario> --ship <id> --difficulty <level> [--soak-pilot ai|idle] plays the battle without a window and reports how long the player lasted, hits taken and enemies destroyed; the default campaign's balance promise is held by a unit test of the same runner (soak).
    • Hazards and environments: the testbed scenarios hazard-asteroids, hazard-black-hole, hazard-minefield, env-orbit, env-pocket, surface-moon, surface-hills, surface-colony and surface-strike (default) set each up on its own; --soak <scenario> --soak-pilot ai shows whether craft survive them. A craft "vanishing" in an atmosphere has usually climbed through the ceiling (the message log says LEFT THE ATMOSPHERE). Physics questions make good unit tests: sim::env's tests build an Env with a flat ground by hand.
    • Missions sit above the simulation: mission::MissionRun::update runs after each World::step and spawns through World::spawn_placement (Missions); sim itself knows nothing about them.
