    Cockpit and HUD¶

    src/hud/: the 2D layer over the 3D view. hud/mod.rs holds the pure logic (tested); hud/draw.rs paints it with macroquad (verified by manual tests). hud/layout.rs holds the pure maths of 3D-cockpit anchors and the cockpit layout editor. Plan: Stage 1; Stage 2 added the power and systems instruments, status flags, the MFD Systems page, text fitted to each instrument, instruments anchored in perspective on a 3D cockpit and the layout editor (F12).

    Architecture Overview¶

    flowchart LR
    W["World + player ShipId"] --> L["pure logic<br/>radar_point, gauge_values, gauge_angle,<br/>mfd_lines, MessageLog::visible,<br/>power_readout, system_lamps, status_flags"]
    CK["CockpitDef.instruments + design<br/>(or default_instruments, 640×480)"] --> LY["Layout::new(screen, design)<br/>design space → pixels"]
    L --> D["draw::draw_targeting<br/>draw::draw_cockpit<br/>draw::draw_instruments<br/>(skips anchored ones)"]
    LY --> D
    AN["CockpitDef.anchors"] --> AC["layout::anchor_corners<br/>(+ model_offset)"]
    AC --> DA["app::flight::draw_anchored<br/>draw_instrument → RenderTarget<br/>→ grid mapped onto the corners"]
    L --> DA

    Anchored instruments (3D cockpits). An anchor (at, normal, size, metres from the eye) is a rectangle lying on the cockpit model's surface. anchor_corners builds its four corners: "up" is world +Y with its component along the normal removed (Gram–Schmidt), "right" completes the frame. Each frame, draw_anchored (in app::flight) projects the corners to measure the instrument's on-screen footprint, draws the instrument with draw::draw_instrument into a RenderTarget at about twice that size (so lines stay sharp), and maps the texture onto the rectangle through a grid of small quads (anchor_point interpolates the corners), because one quad of two triangles would bend straight lines at its diagonal. The instrument then sits in its bezel at any window shape and field of view. HudFrame::anchored names the instruments drawn this way, and draw_instruments skips them.

    Text fitted to instruments. Every instrument painter sizes its text to its box (fit_size: the longest line, measured once at a reference size, scaled to fit the width and the row count to fit the height, capped at a maximum). The MFD uses one size for every page, fitted to the longest page, so text does not jump when pages change. Lines and text also scale with an anchored instrument's texture.

    Design space. Instruments are placed in a cockpit's design space (instruments: {mfd: [x, y, w, h]} in the rules). The original cockpits use 640×480, the default; a cockpit can declare its own with design: [w, h] (the default campaign's Guard cockpit uses 1280×720). Layout::new(screen, design) scales the design space as large as fits (min(screen.w / design.w, screen.h / design.h)) and centres it: a 4:3 cockpit fills the height of a 16:9 screen with space visible at the sides; a 16:9 design fills it exactly. The cockpit image is stretched to the whole design space (Layout::full), whatever its pixel size — which is why an HD repaint (e.g. 2560×1920 replacing a 640×480 original) keeps every instrument aligned without changing a rule.

    Instruments (names used in cockpit rules):

    Name Shows
    radar_front, radar_rear Contacts in the front / rear hemisphere; distance from the centre = angle off the axis (rim = 90°); rear scope mirrored left/right. Faction colours; stations drawn larger; the target boxed. Mobile contacts beyond [flight] RadarRange are hidden.
    mfd One of five pages (MfdPage): Target (name, faction, range, speed, shields, armour, hull, lock state), Weapons (beams, link mode, racks with the selected one marked >), Status (own shields with balance, armour/hull/energy, and the power line BEAM x SHLD y PWR used/budget), Systems (one line per fitted system: OK, ON, DMG n%, OUT, DESTROYED, * while being repaired), Nav.
    gauge1–gauge4 Needles for speed, energy, shields, hull (red below 25 %).
    message The newest message younger than 4 s.
    power B-<level> and S-<level> columns (charge settings), fore/aft shield bars with the favoured facing marked, used/budget in yellow when the budget is full (power_readout).
    systems A grid of lamps, one per fitted system with a three-letter label (system_abbrev): green OK, cyan switched on, amber damaged, red knocked out (blinking while repaired), grey destroyed (system_lamps, Lamp).

    Fallback panels. A cockpit that does not place power or systems gets them as small panels at the top right of its own design space — x from 87.5 % of the design width, y from 9 % of its height — with a dark backdrop over cockpit art. The glass layout (default_instruments) places both along the bottom.

    Ships without cockpit art, and the non-cockpit views, use glass instruments (default_instruments() with dark backgrounds). A throttle/speed/REV/BURN/VCT strip is drawn under the message line, extended with BRAKE while braking and HOLD n while the flight computer holds a speed, and the Stage 2 status flags (status_flags): DISABLED, CLOAKED, TRACTOR, ECM, JUMP n (hyperspace countdown).

    Drift readout (app 0.3.1). The status line reads THR 60% 312 m/s X+45 Y-12: throttle, forward speed, then sideways (X, + right) and vertical (Y, + up) drift in whole metres per second, signed (drift_text). All three come from Ship::body_velocity(), the velocity turned into the ship's own axes (Simulation). In newtonian flight a turn changes the nose, not the motion, so after a hard turn X or Y shows how far the ship is sliding.

    Atmosphere instruments (app 0.6.0). Over a ground, status_line (which now takes the World) appends ALT n VS ±n: draw::altitude(world, ship) — the height above World::ground_height under the craft, None in space — and the world vertical speed. In the forward views draw_targeting draws an artificial horizon: ladder_directions(camera_forward, angles) gives, for each pitch angle, a direction straight ahead in the camera's compass heading (its forward with the vertical part removed) tilted up by that angle. A point 1 km along it is projected, and a line is drawn across it along the camera's level right: 320 m long (at 1 km) for the horizon (0°), 120 m with a +10-style label for the rungs at −20°, −10°, +10°, +20°. Because the directions are built from the level heading, the horizon stays level on screen in world terms whatever the craft's pitch and roll.

    Flight-path marker. draw_targeting projects a point 1 km along the ship's velocity (when faster than 5 m/s) and draws a circle with three ticks (left, right, up) there: where the craft is actually going. With no drift it sits on the crosshair; sideways drift slides it off to the side.

    Targeting (forward views): a reticle; corner brackets sized by the target's angular size; name and range; a lead circle (where to aim beams); a lock-progress ring, then a red lock diamond and LOCK; an arrow on a ring when the target is off-screen. Nav points draw a blue diamond.

    Debugging¶

    • No ALT/VS or horizon: they appear only when the battle has a ground (World::env.ground); in space and in physics: space environments they are hidden by design.
    • Power and systems panels appearing at the top right mean the cockpit has no instruments.power / instruments.systems rectangles; add them to place the instruments.
    • In a 3D cockpit, instruments that drift off their bezels on a narrow window point to a Design that does not match the proportions the bezels were measured at; the field of view widens to fit the design's width (app::flight::cockpit_fov), see Cockpits.
    • Press F12 in the cockpit view to move instruments with the mouse (layout editor, see Cockpits); S saves the result as an addon you can inspect.
    • An anchored instrument that looks mirrored or upside down has its normal pointing into the surface; the editor's ray pick always turns the normal towards the eye.
    • If instruments sit in the wrong place on a cockpit, adjust the instruments rectangles in the cockpit's rules section — no code change needed; positions are in the cockpit's design space (640×480 unless Design says otherwise), not in the image's pixels.
    • The original cockpits' cyan colour key becomes transparent black on load; a cyan halo means the key was not applied (load_image(.., colour_key = true)).
