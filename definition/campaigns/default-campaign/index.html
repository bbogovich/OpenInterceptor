    The default campaign¶

    Status: design; built up stage by stage (plan). First playable content delivered in Stage 2 (resources/default since app 0.4.2, before that packs/default, which replaced the Stage 1 sample pack). Mod id default, kind campaign, no original content. Working title: Perils of the Void.

    It is what the engine runs without the player's original data (an installed copy or the CD), the reference implementation of a complete campaign, and the template a modder copies to make their own. It uses every format construct the interceptor campaign uses, in a simpler form (coverage).

    Aesthetic: a 1950s film serial¶

    The look and the story come from the science-fiction cinema serials and pulp magazine covers of the late 1940s and 1950s — as a genre, not any particular serial or character:

    • Ships: stylised rocket ships (needle and cigar hulls, swept tail fins, nose cones, porthole rows, chrome trim, exhaust bells) against flying saucers (domed discs, rim lights, antenna spikes) and tin-can robots. Low-poly forms with smooth shading and saturated two-tone paint.
    • Palette: rocket-red, cream, chrome and teal for the player; violet, acid green and black for the villain; Technicolor nebulae and painted-backdrop planets with rings.
    • Interface: art-deco panels, Bakelite knobs, radar scopes with phosphor green sweeps, Nixie-tube counters, ticker-tape messages; screen titles in poster lettering.
    • Story: told in chapters. Each opens with a title card ("Chapter 4: Trapped in the Ion Storm!") and a narrator's recap. Each ends on a cliffhanger: a special mission that begins at the worst possible moment. The next chapter resolves it.
    • Planets: battles also happen on worlds — jungle planets under violet skies, airless cratered moons — where rockets fly like aircraft: drag slows them, gravity pulls them down, and fights are slower and closer to the ground (environments).
    • Sound: theremin and organ stings, ray-gun zaps, rocket roars, a brassy title fanfare, all synthesised.

    Premise¶

    The inner worlds are powered by cosmium, a glowing crystal found only in the asteroid belts and moons of six frontier systems. The Interplanetary Guard (the player) protects the mining colonies. From a dark moon at the edge of the map, Regent Vexor of the Umbral Dominion has begun building the Eclipse Engine: a colossal lens that, fed with enough cosmium, will cast the inner worlds into permanent night unless they kneel.

    Both sides race to mine the belts, research new technology, build stations and fight for every system. The Regent needs cosmium for his Engine; the Guard needs it to stop him.

    Factions¶

    Faction Role Kind Notes
    Interplanetary Guard The player player Rocket ships; bases with modules
    Umbral Dominion The opponent; its diabolical plan drives the campaign rival Saucers and robots; own economy, research tree, bases, strategist
    Solar Senate Third party: the government patron Funds the Guard per protected colony; can cut funding, demand missions, or (if relations collapse) recognise the Regent and stop funding altogether
    Nebula Cartel Third party: smugglers and traders independent Sells rare parts to whoever pays; secretly supplies the Dominion while relations with the Guard are poor; can be turned into an ally or broken
    Belt Prospectors Third party: free miners independent Their mines yield cosmium to whoever protects them
    Black Comet raiders Third party: pirates hostile Raid everyone; may be hired by the Cartel or the Regent

    Relations are dynamic (format). Defending a faction's bases, convoys and ships raises its standing with the Guard; attacking them lowers it; and each faction's enemies react the opposite way. Over the campaign the player can make the Cartel an ally (its convoys then supply the Guard and stop supplying the Regent), lose the Senate's backing by neglecting its colonies, or drive the Prospectors into the Regent's arms. Stances change who fights alongside or against the player in battle, who trades, and which story branches open.

    Structure: chapters driven by both sides' progress¶

    The campaign is one arc of 12 chapters. A chapter's cliffhanger mission is triggered by conditions on the player's progress and the Regent's plan, not by the calendar alone, so a strong player meets the story faster and a struggling one sees the villain advance.

    The Eclipse Engine is a plan with three stages (Lens, Coils, Heart). Each stage needs shipments of cosmium and parts carried by saucer convoys, which the player can intercept, and the plan's progress is visible to every chapter condition.

    Ch. Title (working) Cliffhanger triggered when Special mission Outcome
    1 The Shadow Falls Campaign start Tutorial defence: saucers strike a mining colony Unlocks the star map, first base
    2 Raiders of the Belt First mine built, or day 15 Convoy escort through raider space Prospectors' relation; cosmium income
    3 The Regent's Ultimatum Dominion builds its 2nd base, or Guard researches 4 topics Broadcast intercept: catch a saucer carrying the Regent's message Reveals the Eclipse Engine; plan stage 1 begins
    4 Trapped in the Ion Storm! Plan ≥ 15% and a Guard squadron is in deep space Survive: an ion storm cripples sensors while saucers attack Storm map hazard learned; derelict found
    5 The Secret of the Derelict Chapter 4 done Surface salvage: the derelict has crashed on a jungle planet; fly down through the atmosphere and tractor it out under fire from saucers and ground robots Research: saucer propulsion
    6 Senate in Peril Senate relation < 20 or plan ≥ 33% Defend the Senate station from a robot boarding fleet Senate relation ±; funding changes
    7 The Cartel's Price Cartel relation crosses friendly or wary Choice: escort a Cartel shipment (Cartel ally) or raid it (cut the Regent's supply) Branches: Cartel ally or Cartel hostile
    8 Lens of Doom Plan stage 1 complete Strike the lens assembly yard Delays stage 2 (or loses a colony)
    9 The Traitor in the Guard Guard researches saucer propulsion and Dominion research ≥ level 2 Pursuit: chase a defector's stolen rocket before it reaches the Regent One rival research topic stolen or kept
    10 Into the Dark Moon's Shadow Plan ≥ 66% or Guard holds 4 systems Surface recon: fly low over the dark moon's airless craters, under the fortress guns, to photograph the Engine site Map of the Engine site

    Victory: destroy the Eclipse Engine (total victory if the flagship survives, partial if it is lost). Defeat: the Engine completes; the Guard's last base falls; or the Senate withdraws recognition (funding at 0 for 60 days).

    Logistics: mines, research stations and foundries (planned)¶

    Features: Strategy & logistics (F-STR-005 – F-STR-009).

    The user has asked for the default campaign's strategy layer to run on supply lines (design; decisions recorded 2026-11-11):

    • New base types: mining facility, research station, manufacturing center, built in star systems according to their resources; research stations only in systems meeting a research site's criteria (a nebula, a pulsar…).
    • Supply and demand: the main base orders components (for fighters and other technology) from manufacturing centers; manufacturing orders materials from mining facilities; nothing is produced until its materials have arrived. Resources and components are campaign data.
    • Supply convoys are automatic, provided by the government, and travel the fixed lanes as convoys of several large vessels (a core vessel with attached cargo containers), each carrying an even share of the cargo, so a partly destroyed convoy still delivers the surviving share. Where along its route a convoy will be attacked is chosen at random at launch; destroyed cargo is lost, captured cargo goes to the Dominion's supply depots (destroy a depot to seize it).
    • Research stations open tech branches that ordinary bases cannot research (for example theremin shielding at a nebula lab); materials are needed only to begin a project.
    • Survey ships — a different mission and craft from supply ships — are sent to systems within the base's radar range to find which station types they suit; when one launches, a 25 % roll decides whether it will be attacked.
    • Fighters are built at main bases and transferred to a facility's own pool, defending it and escorting its supply ships.
    • Construction takes time and passes through three construction stages, each with its own mesh and debris (implemented: build_25 from 0 %, build_60 from 60 %, build_90 from 90 %, thresholds confirmed); from 50 % the Dominion may attack a facility under construction, at most three times per location.
    • Third-party convoys: Prospectors fly expeditions (to a system, pause, home); the Cartel's destinations are random, even uncharted stars.

    This fits the premise — both sides race to mine the belts — and gives the Escort and Convoy raid mission types a strategic cause. The Regent's Eclipse Engine shipments use the same convoys. Owners: Stage 4 (economy and logistics), Stage 3 (convoy-ambush and construction-defence missions), Stage 5 (the Dominion's depots and third-party convoys). Already built in Stage 2: the facility and supply-craft objects, their construction-stage models and debris, and the construction-defence and convoy-ambush testbed arenas. Still open: supply-vessel cargo capacity (TBD, set by tech-tree balance) (design page). The interceptor campaign stays without logistics.

    Generated mission types¶

    Type Goal
    Intercept Destroy a Dominion strike group before it reaches a colony
    Defend Hold a base, colony, mine or allied station
    Escort Bring a convoy (prospector, Cartel or Senate) through — and, with logistics, the Guard's own supply rockets (convoy_ambush)
    Construction defence Hold a facility under construction against a Dominion strike (logistics, planned)
    Convoy raid Destroy or capture Dominion cargo orbs (slows the Engine)
    Strike Destroy a Dominion mine, pod or base
    Patrol Sweep a system; random contacts
    Salvage Tractor home a disabled saucer or derelict
    Survey Scan a body to reveal its cosmium
    Raider hunt Clear a raider nest (relation effects on Cartel and Prospectors)
    Colony defence (surface) Defend a mining colony or research outpost on a planet: atmospheric flight, gravity, anti-air guns, terrain
    Ground raid Destroy a Dominion surface mine or research outpost

    Assets: all generated by this project¶

    Asset How Tool
    Rockets, saucers, robots, stations, modules, probes, mines Procedural low-poly OBJ from parametric parts: lathed hulls (rocket bodies, saucer discs, domes), fins, portholes, antennae, rings; LODs, slots and overlay groups tools/default_assets/models.py (replaced the Stage 1 make_sample_models.py)
    Debris families Each model cut procedurally into 3–5 pieces along planes, giving the Piece.n models same tool
    Textures Procedural two-tone paint with chrome trim, rivets, porthole glow, faction insignia, scorch overlays for damage states tools/default_assets/textures.py (PNG, standard library)
    Cockpits A 3D cockpit per rocket (chrome dash, round dials, radar scope) and one 2D art-deco cockpit image models.py / textures.py
    UI art Art-deco panels, buttons, dials, chapter title cards, poster-style screen titles, star map glyphs tools/default_assets/ui.py
    Sounds Synthesised: ray zaps, rocket roar, saucer hum, explosions, theremin and organ stings, UI clicks tools/default_assets/sounds.py (WAV)
    Music A title fanfare, an exploration loop and a battle loop, synthesised sounds.py
    Text Chapter cards and narrator recaps, briefings, encyclopaedia, messages, names: written for the project in a pulp register, in text/en/ by hand

    All tools are Python standard library only and deterministic (fixed seeds); their outputs are committed so the mod works without running them. Everything is MIT-licensed with the engine. Names, characters and designs are original to this project; the serial genre is the inspiration, not any specific work.

    Delivered so far (Stage 2, first delivery)¶

    python3 tools/default_assets/make_all.py regenerates everything below (--check builds twice and proves the output is byte-identical); common.py validates every model (closed solids wound outward, no degenerate faces, triangle budgets) before it is written.

    Asset Delivered
    Ships Guard rockets Comet, Meteor and Thunderbolt (playable) and the Victory; Dominion scout and raider saucers, the Dreadnought disc, the robot sentinel, the Grappler and the cargo orb; Senate patrol rocket, Cartel freighter, Prospector hauler, Black Comet hot-rod
    Stations Guard station, Dominion outpost, cosmium asteroid
    Debris Three to five pieces per class, plus generic rubble chunks
    Textures Hull paint per faction plus scorched and burnt damage variants
    Cockpit The 3D art-deco Guard cockpit guard (no 2D art), with bezels for every instrument including power and systems; rectangles measured by projecting the bezels with the cockpit camera
    Sounds Synthesised weapon, explosion, hit, lock, afterburner, saucer hum, theremin sting, UI, and Stage 2 system sounds (system damage, tractor, cloak, hyperspace; ECM reuses the target click)
    Rules YAML (mod format 2): seven factions, six beams and two missiles, systems and power rules (rules/systems.yaml), equipment fits by systems+, flight rules (hyperspace_time: 8s), behaviours for every enemy type and the Guard pilots (rules/behaviours.yaml) with aim, reaction and fire discipline per level, the campaign ruleset with four difficulty levels (campaign/campaign.yaml; default ranger)
    Cockpit layout The Guard cockpit's instruments are also anchored on the dashboard (drawn in perspective); models/guard_cockpit.instruments.yaml is generated with the model
    Scenarios rocket-vs-saucer, dominion-raid, black-comet-ambush, systems-trial

    Not yet: model sets with damage states, the 2D cockpit, UI art, music, text.

    Built up by stage¶

    Every stage's features must work in the default campaign as well as in interceptor (project rule), so the campaign grows with the engine:

    Stage Default campaign content added
    2 The rockets, saucers and robots with model sets, damage states and debris; behaviours with difficulty variants; weapons and equipment; a "rocket vs saucer" testbed arena
    3 The generated mission types (including the two surface types, with atmospheric flight and terrain) and chapter missions 1, 2, 4 and 5 as playable missions; the condition/action language exercised by objectives
    4 The fixed star map, economy, research trees, bases and modules, purchase and loadout, pilots
    5 Factions with dynamic relations, the Dominion strategist, the Eclipse Engine plan, all 12 chapters: a complete playable campaign
    6 Screens skinned with the art-deco UI, chapter title cards, music, narrator text
    7 Balance pass, complete encyclopaedia, the modding tutorial built on this campaign
