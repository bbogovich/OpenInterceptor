# Deviations

Where OpenInterceptor knowingly differs from the original game, or uses an engine value where
the original's is unknown, and why. A deviation on the record is a decision; an undocumented
one looks like a bug.

| ID | Deviation | Why | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEV-001 | **Default keys for actions the original left undocumented**: `M` match speed, `W` cycle missile, `Kp4/6/8/2` VCT slides, `Q`/`E` roll, `LeftControl` also fires beams, `C` mouse flight. | These actions exist in the original's bindable list but their release defaults are not documented anywhere found; Q/E and LeftControl help keyboards without a keypad. | 1 |
| DEV-002 | **Navigation points** in scenarios (`nav: {<name>: …}`), cycled with `N`, shown on the MFD Nav page and as an on-screen marker. | The original has no in-mission nav points (travel happens on the strategy map). Mods need them; the test arenas use them to find the alien and pirate groups. | 1 |
| DEV-003 | **Gamepad** support with an XInput-style layout. | The original supported DirectInput joysticks and rudders only. | 1 |
| DEV-004 | **Mouse controls are rebindable** (beams/missile via the bindings; steering range via `mouse_range` in `bindings.yaml`) and mouse flight is a toggle (`C`). | The original fixed them; a toggle avoids the mouse fighting the menus. Steering direction follows the readme (cursor down = nose down). | 1 |
| DEV-005 | **Hardware 3D with per-vertex lighting** instead of the 16 shade palettes. | The shade palettes served the 1998 software renderer; palette 0 (full brightness) gives the texture colours. | 1 |
| DEV-006 | **Wide-screen**: a cockpit's design space (640×480 for the originals) is scaled as large as fits and centred; the 3D view fills the window. Cockpits may declare their own `design` (e.g. 1920×1080) and may be 3D models. | Modern displays are not 4:3; modders replace cockpits with HD, widescreen or 3D ones. | 1 |
| DEV-007 | **Multiplayer** deferred, possibly never implemented. | Not needed for single player or modding. | 7 |
| DEV-008 | **Original beam `Range` read as kilometres** (`Range=4` in `GameSettings.ini` → 4 000 m, when inherited through `inherit: original:…`). Since mod format 2 a mod's `range` takes a unit (`2.4km`, `350m`; a plain number is metres), replacing the Stage 1 `RangeMetres` key. | The original's 1–6 values have no documented unit; kilometres matches the documented sensor ranges (3–5 km). | 1 |
| DEV-009 | **Missile `TurnRate` and `LockTime` are engine values** set in the Interceptor pack. | The original stores no turn rate per missile; lock time depends on the targeting system (Stage 4 equipment). | 1 |
| DEV-010 | **Proximity fuse fires on a near miss**: inside `ProximityRadius` *and* moving away from the target. A hit inside the target's collision shape deals `ImpactDamage`. | Firing as soon as the radius was entered meant impacts never happened. | 1 |
| DEV-011 | **Hull `Shields`, `Armor`, `Energy`, `EnergyRecharge`, `Acceleration` are engine values per hull** in the Interceptor pack. | In the original they come from fitted equipment (ShieldType*, ArmorType*, EngineType*), which arrives with the equipment system. | 1 → 4 |
| DEV-012 | **Alien, freighter and capital flight values are estimates** (MaxSpeed, turn rates, acceleration). | The original file has flight values only for the three X-COM hulls; the others were coded per class. | 1 → 2 |
| DEV-013 | **Missile lock cone is 10°** (`flight.lock_angle`). | The original's `SensorsSetting LockAngle` units are not decoded. | 1 |
| DEV-014 | **Cockpit ↔ hull mapping** A = Lightning II, B = Firestar, C = Super Avenger. | Derived (one cockpit per hull), not confirmed. | 1 |
| DEV-015 | **One-shot inputs are carried across frames** that run no simulation tick (`Controls::carry_unconsumed`). | On high-refresh displays some frames run no tick; a key press would otherwise be lost. Not an original behaviour, an engine correctness fix. | 1 |
| DEV-016 | **Esc opens a pause menu** (resume, restart, back to launcher) instead of resetting the MFD. | The testbed needs a way back to the launcher; MFD pages cycle with `<`/`>`. | 1 |
| DEV-017 | **Keyed cockpit pixels become transparent black**, not transparent cyan. | Filtering would otherwise blend cyan into the edges of the art. Visual result matches the original. | 1 |
| DEV-018 | ~~**"Ignore my target" and "retreat" wingman orders both mean "form on me"**; "evasive" is not bound.~~ **Resolved in Stage 2:** every wingman order has its own behaviour (formation, attack my target, fire at will, 10 s evasive weave, retreat and hyperspace out) and evasive is bound (`Shift+E`). | Distinct behaviours arrived with the Stage 2 AI. | 1 → 2 |
| DEV-019 | **Newtonian flight model by default**: throttle is thrust, ships coast at zero throttle, `Z` reverses thrust, a held **brake** (`B`) retro-thrusts to a stop, `MaxSpeed` caps speed, turning does not steer the velocity. `flight: {model: assisted}` restores the arcade model (throttle = target speed, automatic slowing, drift damped). | The original's flight model is not documented and felt arcade-like; the project owner chose physics where deceleration only comes from thrust. Keeping both as data lets each campaign choose. | 1 |
| DEV-020 | **Flight computer (speed hold)**: match speed (`M`) and every AI pilot fly a held forward speed through `hold_speed`, which thrusts and brakes within the hull's own acceleration and also cancels sideways drift. | Under newtonian physics AI pilots need a way to manage speed that obeys the same limits as the player; cancelling drift keeps AI dogfights readable. | 1 → 2 |
| DEV-021 | **Arena edge**: ships reaching `flight.arena_radius` (default 60 km) are halted against it ("ARENA BOUNDARY"). | Under newtonian physics a coasting ship would otherwise drift away for ever; outside testing this should rarely be reached. | 1 |
| DEV-023 | **Overlay polygons are depth-biased** instead of drawn in BSP order; coplanar overlapping faces are found geometrically, the original overlay flag only decides which is on top. Translucent-flagged polygons are drawn opaque for now. | A depth-buffered renderer cannot use painter's order per model; the same pass then also serves mod OBJ decals, which have no flag. The opacity meaning of the flag is not confirmed. | 1 |
| DEV-022 | **Per-asset overrides and `--extract`**: any original model, texture, image or sound is replaced by a pack file at its override path; the extractor writes every original asset at those paths in OBJ/PNG/WAV. | The modding objective: HD and 3D replacements without touching or redistributing the originals (compare XWVM, [Modding](../../modding/index.md#comparison-with-xwvm)). | 1 |
| DEV-024 | **Power budget and start levels are engine values**: the beam and shield charge settings plus switched-on equipment share `power.budget: 6` steps; ships start at beam D / shield C (`interceptor`) or C / C (`default`); tractor, cloak and ECM use 2, 3 and 1 steps. | The original's charge-rate tables are verified (`BeamRechargeRate`, `ShieldRechargeRate`), but how many levels may be high at once and what equipment costs is not in the data (derived). All are `power` / `systems` data. | 2 |
| DEV-025 | **Firing drops the cloak** (beams or a missile), with the message "CLOAK DROPPED". | Prevents an invisible ship from attacking with impunity; the original's exact rule is unknown. Cloak lock-break by difficulty level waits for 2.8. | 2 |
| DEV-026 | **Hyperspace countdown** of `flight.hyperspace_time` seconds (10 in `interceptor`, 8 in `default`), doubled while the hyperdrive is damaged; pressing `H` again aborts. | The original's countdown length is not documented (derived). | 2 |
| DEV-027 | **Default keys for tractor, cloak and ECM**: `Shift+T`, `Shift+C`, `Shift+J`. | The original's key list has no documented default for these; Shift chords keep the plain `T`, `C` and `J` free. Rebindable in the controls menu. | 2 |
| DEV-028 | **Gamepad modifier layer**: holding the left shoulder (`LeftShoulder`) switches the pad to a second layer (`LeftShoulder+A` hyperspace, `LeftShoulder+DpadUp` power to beams, …); a button used as a modifier does nothing on its own. | A controller has far fewer buttons than the keyboard has keys; chords make every action reachable from the pad (extends DEV-003). | 2 |
| DEV-029 | **System condition thresholds**: above half its hit points a system works fully, at half or below at half effect, at zero it stops until repaired back to 25 %, below `−destroy_below × hit_points` (−50 %) it is destroyed for the battle. Repair takes the worst system first. | The original shows damaged / inoperative / destroyed systems and repairs them, but its thresholds and repair order are not in the data (derived). Thresholds below zero and the repair rate are `damage` data. | 2 |
| DEV-030 | **Mod files are a strict subset of YAML**, read by an in-crate reader (`formats::yaml`): no tabs, anchors (`&a`), aliases (`*a`), tags (`!!str`), multiple documents, complex keys (`? `) or plain scalars continued over several lines — each rejected with its line number. Every scalar stays text until the schema types it. Mod files moved from INI (format 1, no longer read) to YAML (format 2). | YAML gives modders nesting, lists and comments that INI lacked, and is what OpenXcom modders know. A full YAML library was not taken: the project's dependency policy avoids dependencies for what the codebase can do, `serde_yaml` is unmaintained, and full YAML's implicit typing (`no` → false, `1.10` → 1.1) and anchors surprise modders. The subset covers every construct the mod files need; the original game's own INI files are still read as INI. | 2 |
| DEV-031 | **AI skill model**: enemies aim with an error of up to `(1 − aim) × 8°`, re-picked every `reaction` seconds, fire in `burst`/`pause` cycles inside a `fire_cone`, and check behaviour transitions 4 times a second. | The original's AI is hard-coded and its accuracy model unknown; this model is data, so each campaign tunes it per difficulty level. | 2 |
| DEV-032 | **Interceptor enemy behaviours and per-level values are derived** (Wraith, Spectre, Phantasm, Shade, Harbinger, drones, pirates, corporates, X-COM wingmen; skill, bursts, missile intervals and flee thresholds for beginner … superhuman). | The original's AI code is not data; the behaviours follow the strategy guide's descriptions of each class and were tuned in the testbed (and with `--soak`). What each original difficulty level changes in combat is unknown. | 2 |
| DEV-033 | **3D cockpit instruments drawn in perspective on the dashboard** (anchors) and an in-game **cockpit layout editor** (F12) that saves an addon. | Engine extensions for modding: the original has only 2D cockpits. XWVM's 3D cockpits show instruments on in-cockpit screens the same way. | 2 |
| DEV-034 | **Explosions are engine fireballs** (size, duration, colour, optional shock-wave ring per effect) instead of the original's XSPT sprite animations; which original wreck set each class uses follows the set names. | XSPT pixel runs are not decoded yet; the 28 wreck sets' names and chunk meshes are verified from `Interceptor.exe`, the class mapping is derived. Effects are data, so sprite explosions can replace the fireballs later without rule changes. | 2 |
| DEV-035 | **Module placement on the original bases is derived**: which of the X-COM base's 33 and the alien base's 28 attachment points carries a turret, shield generator, hangar or pod. Hit points come from the original `M_XCOM*` / `M_ALIEN*` sections (verified); a station's shields fail once all its shield-generator modules are destroyed, and destroying the hub destroys its modules. | The original assembles bases on the strategy layer (Stage 4), so their battle layout is not stored; the shield and cascade rules follow the strategy guide's descriptions (derived). Placement is `modules: [{slot, class}]` data. | 2 |
| DEV-036 | **Special missile behaviours are engine models of the original's descriptions**: Sidestep turns twice as hard inside 600 m and weaves; Parallax decoys lure at 80 % per second within 2.5 km; Passive Cloaking waits for prey within 800 m; Doppelganger copies are fragile and short-lived; mines blast four times their fuse radius. | The original's guidance code is not data; names and one-line descriptions are all that is known (derived). Every number is a missile key or an engine constant documented on *Missiles and mines*. | 2 |
| DEV-037 | **Shield rings on the radar scopes** (front scope = fore shield, rear = aft; green / amber / red, blinking when down) and **top-bar text placement** in the `interceptor` cockpits (message in the lit band, throttle line, power and systems in the two strips below). | The rectangles are measured from the cockpit art (verified pixel bounds); what the original showed in each strip and the ring thresholds are derived from the player's description of the original HUD. | 2 |
| DEV-038 | **Point-defence data is derived**: which ships carry point defence (the Patton, the X-COM freighter, the bases; the dreadnought and the stations) and its numbers (range 900 m–1.2 km, 3–4 shots/s, 35–40 % kill chance, 3–4 energy per shot). Point defence also shoots at decoys. | The original's point-defence behaviour is not data; the values are engine estimates tuned in the testbed. All are `point_defence` keys. | 2 |
| DEV-039 | **Probes act in battle**: a sensor probe extends its side's radar to 8 km around it; an ECM / jamming probe halves hostile radar range and missile-lock speed within 2.5 km. | The original launches probes from the strategy map; their battle effects and numbers are derived (mesh names `SENSPROB`, `ECMPROB` verified). Data: `probe: {sensors, jamming}`. | 2 |
| DEV-040 | **Mission arrivals belong to the role** (`at: {distance, bearing, from, spread}` and `arrive: <condition>`) instead of a separate `arrivals:` map as first designed. | One place per group keeps a role's composition, position and timing together; a role without `arrive:` enters at the start. Waves add members later. | 3 |
| DEV-041 | **Interceptor mission compositions are derived**: the five implemented mission types (`xcom_base_defense`, `defend_freighter_convoy`, `deep_space_encounter`, `attack_alien_outpost`, `destroy_alien_probe`) use the original's own names, briefings and debriefs by key (`@orig.*`, read from the CD), but which classes appear, how many, where and in what waves are estimates, sized early-war so every level is winnable and losable (checked with `--soak`). | The original composes battles in code and grows them with campaign progress (Stage 4); compositions are `missions:` data. `%s` in original briefings awaits the strategy layer. | 3 |
| DEV-042 | **Process: Rapid Prototyping run as an implementation project.** The project follows the Rapid Prototyping workflow (SpecItOut proposal P65), but until `sio` supports a `prototype` kind the manifest declares `kind: implementation`, with `spec` pointing at this worktree's `docs/specification` (Definition and Specification 1.0.0). | The `sio` client does not yet support the prototype kind; the implementation kind lets issues cite requirement IDs now. | Process |
| DEV-043 | **Interceptor shots use plain physics** (`flight: {bolt_frame: world}`): a bolt leaves with the ship's velocity plus the muzzle's turn velocity plus its muzzle speed, then flies straight. | Engine value: how the original moves its shots has not been observed side by side; physics is the default by decision D1 (R-CMB-021). `bolt_frame: ship` is the arcade alternative, one rules line away. | 3 |
| DEV-044 | **Interceptor lighting adds a rim light and distant dots** (`flight: {lighting: {ambient: 0.30, sun: 0.85, rim: 0.35, distant_dot: 4}}`): hulls seen edge-on are outlined, and craft smaller than 4 px on screen that are on the radar are drawn as a dot in their radar colour. | An engine choice for visibility (F-HUD-007: "it is difficult to see other fighters"). Ambient and sun match the engine's earlier fixed lighting; the original's look is otherwise unchanged and the keys are data. | 3 |
| DEV-045 | **Interceptor hazards are engine models with derived numbers**: the black hole pulls with 1/d² (`pull` at 1 km, capped at 20 ×) and destroys craft inside its `radius`; the ion storm keeps a fraction of radar range and halves lock speed; asteroid fields are scattered, slowly turning objects. The `interceptor` testbed scenarios use `pull: 12`, `radius: 450` and `sensors: 40%`. | The original's belt and black-hole battles are built in code; their pull law, numbers and sensor effects have not been measured. All are `hazards:` data (R-MSN-009). | 3 |
| DEV-046 | **The strategy format as built is a simpler subset of its design**: one `start:` section instead of `starts`; `base_types.max_distance` (map units) instead of a `placement` expression; plain numbers for `upkeep`, `maintenance`, `salary` and costs (only incomes, expenses, the research rate and prerequisites are expressions); `raids:` with fixed missions instead of squadrons, flight plans and generated missions; pilot ranks by experience thresholds; fitting is *best available* from the store (app 0.7.2) without fitting rules. | Stage 4 first makes every rule playable end to end with the default campaign; the richer keys of [Format: strategy layer](../../specification/design/format-strategy.md) are added when the `interceptor` strategy data (4.12) and logistics (4.14 – 4.17) need them. | 4 |
| DEV-047 | **The default campaign's strategy texts are plain English** in `resources/default/strategy/*.yaml`, not text keys. | The strategy layer was written ahead of its translation; moving the names and descriptions to `text/en/` keys is a data change for a later pass (R-STR-010 Open note). | 4 |
| DEV-048 | **A battle runs on after its result**: the verdict and computer voice come at once, and the debrief waits `flight.debrief_delay` (30 s by default; Enter skips it; a retreat shows it at once). | User request (playtest, app 0.7.4): to watch the final explosion. An engine choice: the original's own timing is not measured here (*unknown*); the delay is rules data, so a campaign can set `0s`. | 3 |
| DEV-049 | **Logistics as built simplifies its design**: facility placement is a **fixed rule per role** (mining: a surveyed system with deposits; research: a surveyed research site; manufacturing: at most two lanes from a base; one facility of each type per system) instead of the design's `placement:` expressions; the nearest supplier is measured in a **straight line** between systems, not by lane (R-STR-013); convoy ambushes are **auto-resolved** as per-vessel losses (since app 0.9.2 only when the campaign has no `logistics.ambush_mission` or the player declines to fly the escort), and an attacked survey ship is lost on a fixed 50 % roll, instead of generating battles; `storage` limits only mining facilities. | App 0.9.0 (`ff9d8fc`): the smallest version of the design that plays the whole chain. Expressions over the campaign context and flown ambushes (`convoy_ambush` exists) are the next steps; the survey loss chance should become data. Default campaign only; the `interceptor` campaign has no logistics. | 4 |
| DEV-050 | **The `interceptor` strategy layer's estimates are derived**: the start (date, pilots, craft, stock), the generated map's number of systems, delivery and salvage, hull speeds, missile `rounds`, module groups and effects, the X-COM base type's cost and hub sensor range, corporate funding and the raid rolls and targets are authored in `packs/interceptor/strategy/campaign.yaml`, each marked `DERIVED`; the pilot rules and mission score points are authored there too, from the design analysis, without the mark. The **tech-id table** in `references.yaml` is read from the player's executable by a layout that is itself *derived* (12-byte entries: `R_` name pointer, string id, tech id); ids 75 and up are not in it and are left out of conditions; research `unlocks` are not authored (items, hulls and modules are gated by their own `TechRequired`). | App 0.8.0 (`c64d178`, plan item 4.12): research sizes and prerequisites, costs, production times and maintenance are inherited from the player's `GameSettings.ini` (*verified* as data, no values in the pack); what the original keeps in code or its manual describes only loosely is estimated, from the design analysis. **Not play-tested**: a six-month automatic run researches 4 of 71 topics on one downlink. The original's monthly evaluation per outpost and the strategist replace the funding and raid estimates in Stage 5. | 4 |
| DEV-051 | **The story format as built is a simpler subset of its design** (app 0.10.0): `arc: <id>` names the arc (the step is its place in `steps`) instead of `{id, step}`; the actions are the fourteen of `EVENT_ACTIONS` — `mission(x)` offers a mission now (no `start_mission` with a place and an expiry, no `offer_mission`, no `reward:`), no map changes; plans have stages of `{name, count}`, a shipment `chance` instead of `ship_visibility`, warnings as a list of `{at, do}`, no `budget`, `grant_if_missed` or `plan_stage(x)`; relations are the **player's standing with each faction** only (no pairs between other factions, no `relation_rules`, no `scale`), and a stance change is a notice, not an `on_stance_change` event; of the stance effects only *hostile or not* acts (raids and battle hostility) — no allies in battle, no trade effects; the strategist is **aggression only** (`strategist:` with `aggression` and `calm_after_wins`, not `strategists.<id>` with budgets, expansion and an offence ladder); endings have `text` and `victory` only (no image, video, music or `continue`). | Stage 5 first makes the story playable end to end in both campaigns with the smallest format that expresses their arcs, plans and endings; the rest of the design stays the target (R-CPN-007 – R-CPN-010 notes). | 5 |
| DEV-052 | **The story content is incomplete and partly derived** (app 0.10.1 – 0.10.2): the default campaign's chapters fly the nearest existing mission where their bespoke special mission is not built (only the finale `eclipse_assault` is its own); the `interceptor` arc's gates use the `type_1/2/3_saucer_examination` topics for the guide's Wraith, Spectre and Phantasm examinations (*derived*, unconfirmed), its later first-message day, middle-level plan counts, shipment interval, side-mission triggers and strategist values are *derived*, and only two of the seven side missions exist. | The arcs, plans and endings are what the engine needed to prove; the missing missions are content work, recorded on R-CPN-004 and R-CPN-005. | 5 |
