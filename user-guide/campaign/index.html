    Campaign¶

    A campaign with a strategy layer is played on the strategy screen: a star map with your bases and the enemy's raids, a clock, and panels for research, building, buying, making, craft and pilots (app 0.7.1 – 0.9.0), and — in the default campaign — a supply chain of mines, factories and research stations (app 0.9.0). When your craft meet a raid you fly the battle; the debrief brings you back to the map. The default campaign, Perils of the Void, has one; so does the interceptor campaign since app 0.8.0, built from your own copy's data (not yet play-tested, and without the supply chain, which the original does not have).

    The screen is functional and plain in this version: every rule is playable, but it is not yet dressed in the campaign's look (that is Stage 6).

    Starting¶

    • In the launcher, a campaign with a strategy layer lists Campaign: continue and Campaign: new game first, and the selection starts on continue.
    • New game starts at the launcher's difficulty level. Continue loads the campaign's save (there is one save per campaign); with no save, the launcher shows the error.
    • From the command line: openinterceptor --no-original --strategy opens a new default campaign directly (Getting started).

    • Top line: the date, the clock speed, your funds and the campaign score.
    • Map (left): star systems (hidden ones appear when your sensors reach them), lanes, your bases with their sensor ranges, enemy contacts once detected, and your craft in flight. With a supply chain also: your facilities (a small diamond beside the system, grey while it is being built, orange when working), supply convoys (gold dots) and survey ships (sky-blue rings).
    • Panel (right): nine tabs, each a list of rows. The current base is marked * on the Bases tab; orders apply to it.

    The clock stops by itself whenever something needs you: a building or base is finished, research is complete, a contact is detected, a raid strikes, the monthly report, an interception reaching its contact, or — with a supply chain — a facility completed, a survey's report or a convoy ambushed. The message is shown in a banner; Enter, Esc or a click dismisses it.

    Interceptions and battles¶

    1. A raid is detected: the clock stops with Contact: , heading for .
    2. On the Contacts tab, Enter on it sends your ready craft. They fly to it on the map — along the lanes to the system nearest it, then straight to it (app 0.7.3). Raiders, too, come in along the lanes, and the map draws each one's way ahead.
    3. When they reach it, the clock stops and a banner asks: Enter to fly the battle, Esc to break off (the contact flies on).
    4. The battle is the raid's mission, with the first craft's hull as your ship and its fitted weapons (app 0.7.2). Fly it as usual. When the result is decided the battle runs on until the debrief (Debrief); at the debrief, Enter or Esc returns to the map. Leaving from the pause menu (L or Q) counts as a retreat.
    5. Back on the map, a banner gives the result and the kills. The outcome's score and money apply; the leader gains the kills and experience; if you were shot down, the craft is lost and its pilot ejected injured — or was killed.

    A raid you do not stop strikes its target: a base loses its newest finished module, and the score drops.

    Supply¶

    Default campaign, app 0.9.0. The Guard builds its craft from components — hull plating, rocket motors, ray emitters — made from mined resources. When you order a hull on the Factory tab, the base orders the components it lacks; a manufacturing center makes them from resources it orders in turn from your mining facilities; everything is carried by automatic government supply convoys along the lanes. You do not move the convoys; you decide where to survey and what to build.

    1. Survey. Click a system on the map, open the Supply tab and choose Survey from . The system must be within the current base's radar range. The survey ship flies there (a sky-blue ring); when it arrives a message lists the deposits it found, with their rates a day, and any research site. A survey ship may be attacked on the way: it fights off the attack or is lost (then survey again).
    2. Build. With a system chosen, the Supply tab offers each kind of facility with its cost:
    3. a mining facility needs a surveyed system with deposits;
    4. a manufacturing center must be within two lanes of one of your bases;
    5. a research station needs a surveyed system that is a research site.

    The row says why when it cannot be built. A facility takes days to build (a grey diamond on the map) and a message says when it is complete. 3. Make. Queue a hull on the Factory tab as usual. Its row waits until the components have arrived; the Supply tab shows the base's store and the orders and convoys under way. 4. Research at a station. A finished research station lists the station-only topics of its kind (Research Theremin shielding here); a topic is refused when the station is not on its research site or its prerequisites are not known. The station first orders the topic's start materials; once they arrive it researches on its own.

    Convoys can be ambushed on the way. In this version you do not fly those battles: each vessel may be lost, a message says how many, and the survivors deliver their share; what is missing is simply ordered again.

    Money and the monthly report¶

    Incomes (the default campaign's Senate stipend grows with your bases and your month's score) and expenses are paid on their own schedules and logged. Every 30 days the monthly report charges module maintenance, craft upkeep and pilot salaries, shows the month's score, the total and your funds, and a new set of recruits is offered.

    Saves¶

    • F5 saves; Esc saves and leaves. The save is <user data folder>/saves/<campaign>.yaml (for example saves/default.yaml); the user data folder is described on Settings.
    • A save records the packs it was played with. Continuing refuses a save that needs a pack that is not active, or one whose rules no longer fit it (a mod was changed), with a message.

    Testbed cheats¶

    The testbed keeps the original game's own cheat list as debug keys on the strategy screen (app 0.8.1). Each says so in the banner and the message log.

    Key Cheat Does
    Ctrl+M payday A million more in funds.
    Ctrl+K knowitall Every research topic known; bases stop researching.
    Ctrl+B quickbase Every base and module under construction finished now (not supply facilities).
    Ctrl+J jump to battle With a contact chosen on the Contacts tab: a ready craft with a pilot is put at it at once, and the fly-or-break-off banner appears. Otherwise it says Choose a contact on the Contacts tab first, or no ready craft with a pilot.

    Limits in app 0.9.0¶

    • Fitting takes the best items in store; you cannot choose them one by one yet.
    • Supply-convoy ambushes are decided automatically; you cannot fly them or escort convoys yet, and facilities have no fighters of their own. The Supply tab is plain text.
    • The interceptor campaign's strategy layer has not been play-tested; its research is slow with one downlink.
    • Fixed in app 0.9.0: manufacturing now costs money as each unit starts, and the default campaign's salvage topics (Saucer hull analysis, Robot brains) can be started after salvaging the right raid.
